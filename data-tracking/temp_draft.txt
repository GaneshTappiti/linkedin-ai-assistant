The .edu email requirement is an American myth.

In India, it locks out 2M+ college students.

Silicon Valley assumes every undergrad gets a campus inbox on day one.
That's false here.
Over 78% of Indian private college students only have a personal Gmail.

If you force an institutional domain, you lock out the hungriest student builders across Tier-2 and Tier-3 campuses before screen one.

We hit that wall immediately.

We couldn't drop verification either.
Unverified campus networks turn into spam boards in 48 hours.
We refused that.

So we built three separate onboarding paths into clstr.in:

→ Tier 1 (Direct Domain): Official college email verified via OTP in 20 seconds.
→ Tier 2 (No-Edu Path): Personal Gmail signup + phone check via libphonenumber + matched against our 367KB registry of non-edu colleges, keeping accounts read-only while we review student IDs.
→ Tier 2 (Spectator Mode): Freshers waiting for an ID card can explore campus clubs and fests without pretending to be verified.

You don't solve trust in India by copying American product playbooks.
You solve it by building for how our colleges actually work.

If you're building for students here, how do you handle the identity gap?
