00 — The Two-Layer Model
How the Clstr Vizag Community team is structured

Why Two Layers — Not One Team

Most communities fail because they try to run everything with one over-stretched team — or they try to hire a full team before they can afford it. Clstr Vizag uses a two-layer model that scales with the community's growth instead of requiring a large team on day one.

LAYER
WHAT IT IS
Layer 1 — Core Ops (founding basis; paid or revenue-share only after actual surplus and written terms)
4 roles. These are the people who make the community function. Events happen because of them. Partnerships are signed because of them. Content goes out because of them. There is no external funding right now — these roles start as founding-team ownership positions, not paid jobs, and move to paid or revenue-share compensation once events start generating surplus.
Layer 2 — Community Volunteers (recognition-based; no fixed cash promise)
3 role types. These are community members who take on defined responsibilities in exchange for recognition, profile status, and early access — not cash. They multiply the team's output without multiplying the payroll.

The rule: Layer 1 handles anything that breaks if a person doesn't show up. Layer 2 handles anything that scales better with more people but doesn't collapse without them.

What each layer owns

LAYER 1 — CORE OPS
LAYER 2 — VOLUNTEERS
Events
Plans, logistics, sponsors, venue, run-of-show
On-ground help, photography, activity facilitation
Content
Strategy, scheduling, quality control, brand voice
Raw content creation, campus-specific posts, UGC
Partnerships
Sponsor pitches, company outreach, MOU negotiations
Warm intros from their own college networks
Community health
Tracks KPIs, resolves issues, onboards new members
Guild/Circle facilitation, member welcome, peer support
Accountability
Reports to Founder. Owned. Named.
Reports to Community/Collab Lead. Light-touch.

The Founder (Ganesh) sits above both layers — not in either. The Founder owns vision, key relationships, and hiring decisions. Once Layer 1 is running well, the Founder's day-to-day involvement in community operations drops to 2 hours per week.

How the team is funded — there is no external investment
Clstr Vizag Community has zero outside funding right now. Every Layer 1 role starts as a founding-team position — not a paid job. Nobody is promised a salary that does not yet exist. Compensation shifts from founding-equity/recognition to paid or revenue-share only once events generate their own surplus.
STAGE
HOW THE TEAM IS COMPENSATED
Phase 0 (Months 1–2)
Founding-team basis only. Recognition, verified badges, resume credit, founder recommendation letters. No cash — there is nothing to pay with yet.
Phase 1 (Months 3–4, first events run)
Event surplus (sponsor income minus event cost) is split by team agreement — not fixed salaries. Amount depends entirely on what a given event actually raises.
Phase 2 onward (community self-funding)
Once event surplus is consistent month over month, the team can formalise a revenue-share or stipend model — reviewed and agreed by the whole team, not promised in advance.

Rule: never promise a fixed cash number to anyone joining Layer 1 or Layer 2 right now. The honest pitch is ownership, resume credit, and a share of whatever the community actually earns — not a salary Clstr cannot yet back.


02 — Hiring Sequence
Who joins when, why that order, and what happens if you hire out of sequence

The Order Matters — Here Is Why

Hiring the wrong role first is how community teams collapse. A Partnerships Manager hired before you have any community to show sponsors is a wasted salary. A Content Lead hired before there is anything happening has nothing to document. The sequence below is designed so each hire has something to work with from day one.

MONTH
WHAT TO HIRE AND WHY
Month 1 — Before anything else
Community/Collab Lead first. This person is the connective tissue. Without them, nothing else coordinates. They set up the systems, onboard ambassadors, and prepare everything for the first event. Hire this role before you hire anyone else — even if it is the only hire you make in Month 1.
Month 2 — Once ambassador system is running
Events & Logistics Coordinator + Brand & Content Lead together. By Month 2, Campus Takeover Series begins (first event). You need someone to run the logistics (Events Coord) and someone to document and promote it (Content Lead). These two roles work in tandem from Day 1 — hire them in the same week.
Month 2 — Alongside Events and Content hires
Campus Node Coordinators recruited from existing ambassadors. These are not new hires — they are promoted from within. The best-performing Campus Captain at each college gets the Community Node Coordinator title and responsibility. Zero extra cost.
Month 3 — Once first events have happened
Guild & Circle Leads recruited from founding cohort members. After the Founding Night (Month 1) and first Campus Takeover (Month 2), you have seen who shows up, who facilitates, who people gravitate toward. Those people become Guild/Circle Leads.
Month 3 — Alongside Guild/Circle leads
Community Hype & Content Creators — 3 to 5 students who naturally create content. Identified from Instagram activity and event documentation. Given a brief and a structured role.
Month 4 — Once community has events track record
Partnerships & Sponsorship Manager. By Month 4, you have 2–3 completed events with documented outcomes (attendance, placements, connections made). That track record is what makes sponsor pitches work. A Partnerships Manager hired before Month 4 is pitching with nothing to show.

If budget forces a choice: Community/Collab Lead is the one non-negotiable hire. Every other Layer 1 role can be delayed by 30–60 days. The Community/Collab Lead cannot be delayed — without them, the ambassador system has no coordinator and the first event has no owner.

07 — Role 05
Guild & Circle Leads  —  Layer 2 Volunteers

Guild & Circle Leads are community members who volunteer to own a specific domain or interest group within Clstr Vizag. They are not paid. They are recognised, badged, and given first access to everything. This is how the community self-organises without the core team doing everything.

The difference between Guilds and Circles
GUILDS (skill/collab focused)
CIRCLES (social/interest focused)
Build Guild — developers, product people, tech builders
Foodie Circle — students who love food, café hunts, food reviews
Design Guild — UI/UX, graphic design, visual creators
Music Circle — musicians, music fans, open jam sessions
Hustle Guild — aspiring founders, business thinkers
Sports Circle — cricket, football, volleyball, beach sports
Career Guild — placement-focused, interview prep, resumes
Travel Circle — weekend trips, city explores, photography walks
Grow Guild — marketing, content, growth, social media
Movie Circle — film reviews, watch parties, short film makers

Why Guilds AND Circles matter: The audit document flagged that the community was entirely collab/utility-framed with almost no zero-agenda social space. Circles fix this. A student who joins the Foodie Circle has no performance bar — they just like food. That student stays in the community because it is fun, not because it is useful.

08 — Role 06
Campus Node Coordinators  —  Layer 2 Volunteers

Campus Node Coordinators are the best-performing Campus Captains from the ambassador program, promoted into a community coordination role. They are not new hires — they are internal promotions. One per active college.

Explicit tasking from the Master Playbook: each Campus Node Coordinator (as Campus Captain before promotion) is tasked with finding at least one person from each Guild and each Circle at their college within their first month. This is what makes the cross-skill, cross-interest promise real instead of accidental — a college with only Build Guild members and no Circle members is not fully covered.

These are the people who were Campus Captains in Phase 0 and hit their milestones. Promoting them into Campus Node Coordinators is the clearest signal Clstr sends that performance has a path — and it costs nothing because they are already in the community.

    • Content submissions per week (target: 2–3 per creator, consistent)
    • Average reach of their featured content on main account
    • FOMO signal: how many non-members DM the community account after their content posts
    • Consistency: minimum 80% of weekly submission targets hit across all creators

10 — Weekly Operating Rhythm
How the team runs week to week — no wasted meetings

The Team's Weekly Calendar

Every meeting in this section has a fixed format, a fixed time limit, and a fixed output. If a meeting produces no decision and no action, it does not happen next week.

FORMAT & RULES

All Layer 1 + Guild Leads + Node Coordinators. Format: KPI review (15 min), upcoming month plan (20 min), open floor for ideas and concerns (25 min). This is the one meeting that should feel like a team.

Rule: Meetings are for decisions, not updates. Updates go in WhatsApp. If a meeting ends without at least 3 decisions made, it was an update disguised as a meeting. Cut it next week.

What everyone earns — recognition, ownership, and career capital. No fixed cash promises.

The Honest Starting Point — No Investment, No Fixed Salaries

Clstr Vizag Community has no external funding right now. Nobody on Layer 1 or Layer 2 is promised a fixed cash amount, because that money does not exist yet. Every role — including the Founder's own time — starts on a founding-team, ownership basis. This is not a lesser offer. It is the honest offer, and it is the one every early team member should be told clearly before joining.

The most common reason student communities stall after a strong start is founder dependency — everything routes through the founder, nothing works when they are unavailable, and the team never develops genuine ownership. This section defines exactly what Ganesh owns permanently, what he delegates to Layer 1 on hiring, and what should be fully team-owned by Month 6.
