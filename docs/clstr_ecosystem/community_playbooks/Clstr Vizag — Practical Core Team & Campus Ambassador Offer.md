# Clstr Vizag — Practical Core Team & Campus Ambassador Offer

**Use this document when recruiting.** Do not send candidates a vague “join our big vision” message. Send them the exact role, work, value and limits below.

**Scope:** Clstr Vizag student community only. Clstr is the community. `clstr.in` is the supporting layer for registration, discovery, participation and continuity.

## 1. The one-line offer

> **Join Clstr to own a real part of Vizag’s student community, build visible proof of leadership, meet students beyond your college and create experiences students genuinely return for.**

This is currently an **unpaid volunteer role**. The exchange is not “work for free and maybe become successful.” The exchange is: **defined responsibility + practical experience + portfolio proof + community access + training + feedback + recognition + progression consideration** in return for a defined amount of contribution.

If Clstr cannot provide those things, it should not recruit for the role.

## 2. The motto and what it means

> **Your college gets you a degree. Clstr gets you a city.**

Clstr helps students meet people beyond their college, find social and skill communities, collaborate, participate in events and grow through the wider student life of Vizag.

> **Find your team. Vizag is the campus.**

The Core Team and Campus Ambassadors help make that promise real. They are not a marketing army and they are not unpaid labour whose only job is to forward posters.

## 3. Exact value provided by Clstr

| Value provided | What the person actually receives |
|---|---|
| Real ownership | A written role, a named area of responsibility and authority to make defined decisions. |
| Portfolio proof | A record of work completed: event, campaign, campus activation, Circle/Guild program, content series or partnership output. |
| Leadership practice | Actual experience coordinating people, deadlines, communication, logistics, content, community or partnerships. |
| Cross-college network | Access to Clstr activities and students from other colleges, subject to capacity and safety rules. |
| Training | One onboarding session plus role-specific guidance and templates. |
| Feedback | A named manager and a monthly review of work, blockers and next responsibility. |
| Recognition | Contribution record, event credits, selected spotlights, a completion certificate and a role-specific recommendation letter when the defined work is completed successfully and documented. |
| Progression | Eligibility to apply for bigger responsibility after meeting the role gate. |
| Influence | A structured way to report what students in that college actually need. |
| Future paid work | Only if a funded task later exists, with separate written terms. It is not guaranteed. |

## 4. What Clstr does not provide

Clstr must say this before selection, not after:

- No salary or fixed stipend at present.
- No guaranteed job or internship.
- No certificate for merely joining. A Clstr completion certificate will be issued after the contributor completes the defined role period and required deliverables.
- No guaranteed access to companies, investors, celebrities or sponsors.
- No automatic equity, revenue share or future payment.
- No promise that the role alone will improve a resume.
- No unlimited availability expectation.
- No payment for spam, fake signups or unverified contact lists.

A candidate who wants immediate cash is not the right candidate for an unpaid role. Do not persuade them otherwise.

## 5. Role A — Campus Ambassador / Campus Captain

### Purpose

Connect one college to the wider Clstr Vizag student community during a defined activation period.

### Time commitment

**2–3 hours per week for four weeks**, with additional time only during an agreed event week. The role is renewed only by mutual agreement.

### Weekly work

| Week | Required output |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Attend onboarding; identify three relevant student groups or clubs; submit a campus pulse note; invite a small number of personally relevant students without spam. |
| Week 2 | Run one approved campus activity: a short introduction, Circle/Guild conversation, interest poll or event orientation; submit attendance and feedback. |
| Week 3 | Bring students into one real Clstr action: event registration, Circle/Guild participation, collaboration or community discussion; report blockers. |
| Week 4 | Complete one activation review: what worked, who participated, what students wanted next and whether the ambassador will continue. |

### Success is not measured by

The number of people added to a WhatsApp group, the number of messages sent, the number of names collected or the number of unverified signups.

### Success is measured by

- completed onboarding;
- accurate communication;
- meaningful verified participation;
- student feedback quality;
- at least one completed campus action;
- reliable reporting;
- respectful conduct;
- willingness to correct weak results.

No fixed numerical target should be promised until Clstr has measured the actual campus response.

### What the Campus Ambassador receives

- role onboarding and a written playbook;
- a defined 90-day activation sprint;
- a contribution record stating exactly what was completed;
- access to relevant Clstr events and community activities where capacity allows;
- feedback from the Ambassador Program Manager or Campus Operations owner;
- eligibility for Campus Node Coordinator review after reliable delivery;
- selected training sessions and cross-college networking;
- a completion certificate after the defined role period and required deliverables are completed;
- a role-specific letter of recommendation when the work is completed successfully, documented and sufficient for a truthful letter;
- public recognition only with the person’s consent.

## 6. Campus Ambassador 90-day milestone rewards

The Campus Ambassador/Campus Captain role is a **90-day sprint** for one college or a small approved campus team. The following are internal campaign targets, not verified market facts. Confirm the targets before each campaign and count only verified students and genuinely active clubs or campus communities.

| Milestone | Internal activation target | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **M01** | 500 verified students and 2 active clubs or campus communities. | Clstr completion certificate. |
| **M02** | 1,000 verified students and 4 active clubs or campus communities. | Role-specific recommendation letter, provided only when the work is documented well enough for a truthful letter. |
| **M03** | 1,500 verified students and all agreed domain clubs/spaces active. | Clstr merchandise, such as a cap or hoodie, subject to stock and size. |
| **M04** | 2,000 verified students and full activation of the agreed campus plan. | Clstr tee, free VIP passes to eligible Clstr events and recognition, subject to capacity, safety, age eligibility and published event rules. |
| **M05** | Top performer at semester end, assessed on verified participation quality, retention, reporting, conduct and community outcome—not raw numbers alone. | Fast-track review for Campus Node Coordinator, Core Team consideration or a funded paid opportunity if one exists; founder appreciation letter and case-study feature with consent. |

The reward order is deliberate: **certificate → recommendation letter → merchandise → tee/VIP passes/recognition → progression and paid-opportunity consideration**. Rewards are not salary. Clstr must publish eligibility before the sprint begins and must never encourage spam, fake signups, purchased accounts, unsafe travel or unauthorised college representation. A free pass does not guarantee transport, food, accommodation or entry to a sold-out, age-restricted or cancelled event.

## 7. Role B — Campus Node Coordinator

### Purpose

Maintain an active and safe Clstr presence at one college after demonstrating reliable Campus Ambassador performance.

### Time commitment

**3–4 hours per week**, reviewed monthly.

### Monthly work

- maintain one active campus communication route;
- support one meaningful Circle, Guild or event action;
- maintain a backup person or succession plan;
- report campus pulse and student feedback;
- support onboarding of new students without spam;
- escalate safety, permission or conduct issues;
- submit a monthly node report.

### Coordinator value

The Coordinator receives deeper ownership, documented distributed-operations experience, priority consideration for event or community leadership, structured feedback and a stronger contribution record. This is not employment and does not guarantee payment.

## 8. Role C — Core Team Lead


### Purpose

Own one function that must work for the community to operate.

### Five-department Core Team structure

The Core Team has five departments. Each department has **two people**, and both must be able to run the department independently. There is no “lead and assistant” structure. The Founder sits above the departments and retains vision, key college and major sponsor relationships, hiring decisions and high-risk approvals. Day-to-day department execution belongs to the department pair.

| Department | Owns | Core seats |
|---|---|---|
| **Logistics** | Events, venue, run-of-show, day-of execution and vendor coordination. | Two Logistics owners. |
| **Design + Social** | Content, brand, Instagram, event creatives and campus-specific handles. | Two Design + Social owners. |
| **Outreach** | Sponsors, companies, colleges and ambassador coordination. | Two Outreach owners. |
| **Tech + Web** | `vizag.clstr.in` upkeep, form fixes, CollabHub support and basic troubleshooting. | Two Tech + Web owners. |
| **Finance + Ticketing** | Event budgets, ticket-sales tracking, sponsor payment records and surplus reporting. | Two Finance + Ticketing owners. |

Community/Collab and Campus Ops may coordinate across departments where required. If a department grows to three people, each person receives a named responsibility. Recurring work must be documented, and primary responsibility should rotate across events so neither person becomes a permanent backup.

### Core Team selection rules

Selection order is **competence first, confidence second, charisma third**. Applicants should state the department they want, provide a portfolio or simple work folder, explain what they want to get from the role, disclose other commitments, provide a weekly-hours number and give references where feasible. Interviewers should use consistent scenario questions and score independently before discussing candidates.

After selection, every Core Team member receives access on day one, one real deliverable in the first month, fixed terms of one semester or one event cycle, monthly feedback and a clean exit route. The two-person rule and documentation prevent one-person dependency.


### Time commitment

**4–6 hours per week for a three-month trial**, agreed before selection. The role can be paused or ended without shame if the commitment is not sustainable.

### Core Team functions

| Function | First 30-day deliverable | Ongoing responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Community / Collab | Design one weekly Circle/Guild rhythm and run a feedback loop. | Member experience, activity between events, collaborations and community health. |
| Events / Logistics | Produce one complete event brief, budget, run sheet and safety checklist. | Venue, vendors, capacity, permissions, safety, check-in and post-event review. |
| Campus Operations | Audit pilot campus nodes and produce a node-health report. | Captains, Coordinators, permissions, backups, succession and campus pulse. |
| Brand / Content | Ship a two-week content plan and three approved student stories or community assets. | Public voice, content quality, creator formats and evidence-based distribution. |
| Partnerships | Build a list of qualified local partners and write one defined pilot proposal. | Partner pipeline, deliverables, reporting, invoices and renewal process. |
| Product Support | Document the top three community friction points in the clstr.in journey. | Feedback, analytics requests, usability issues and reliability/privacy escalation. |

### Core Team value

The Core Team member receives real functional ownership, a portfolio case study, monthly feedback, access to cross-college work, leadership practice, role-specific learning and consideration for higher responsibility. They do not receive an informal promise of salary, equity or future employment.

## 9. What happens in the first 30 days

| Day | Clstr commitment | Contributor output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain mission, role, workload, code of conduct, safety and non-promises. | Confirm fit and availability. |
| 3 | Provide playbook, owner, communication channel and first task. | Accept or decline the task. |
| 7 | Review the first task and remove blockers. | Deliver one small, usable output. |
| 14 | Provide role coaching and feedback. | Deliver the main 14-day output. |
| 21 | Review quality, workload and student value. | Improve or stop the activity if it is not useful. |
| 30 | Conduct a formal review. | Continue, change role, pause or exit. |

If Clstr does not provide the onboarding, owner, feedback and first usable project, it has failed its side of the exchange.

## 10. Recognition and proof system

Recognition must be based on evidence. Each contributor receives a private contribution record containing:

- role and dates;
- agreed responsibilities;
- work delivered;
- measurable result where available;
- feedback from the responsible owner;
- conduct or safety issues if material;
- certificate eligibility;
- recommendation-letter eligibility;
- next progression decision.

Possible public recognition includes event credits, a member spotlight, a leadership badge and a certificate that accurately states the completed contribution. A role-specific letter of recommendation may also be issued when the contributor completes the role successfully, demonstrates reliable conduct and provides enough documented work for the recommender to write a truthful letter. No certificate or recommendation should be issued automatically for merely joining a group.

A recommendation should say what the person actually did. For example: “Coordinated a 90-day campus activation at College X, delivered two approved student activities, reported participation and feedback, and helped 18 verified students complete a Clstr community action.” Use such numbers only when the underlying record exists.

## 11. Progression gates

| From | To | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Applicant | Campus Ambassador | Understands unpaid terms, code of conduct and 90-day commitment. |
| Campus Ambassador | Campus Node Coordinator | Completed the activation, reported honestly, created meaningful participation and maintained reliable communication. |
| Campus Ambassador or Coordinator | Core Team support | Demonstrates ownership, quality, reliability and ability to work across colleges. |
| Core Team trial | Core Team lead | Delivers the 30-day output, communicates blockers and improves a real system. |
| Volunteer role | Paid task | A funded task exists, scope is written, selection is transparent and payment terms are agreed before work begins. |

Progression is not guaranteed. It is evidence-based and depends on actual community needs.

## 12. Reimbursement and paid opportunity rule

If Clstr asks a volunteer to spend money, it must disclose the cost before approval. No volunteer should be required to pay for venue, transport, printing, food, software or event materials without written approval and a clear reimbursement decision.

When cash becomes available, the team may reimburse approved expenses, pay a defined project fee, offer a prize or create a part-time role. Every such arrangement needs written scope, eligibility, amount, timing, approver and payment record.

Do not tell people “you will be paid once we become big.” That is not a benefit; it is speculation.

## 13. Candidate decision test

Tell candidates to join only if all of the following are true:

1. They care about building a better student experience in Vizag.
2. They can commit the stated weekly time.
3. They want responsibility and evidence of work, not only a title.
4. They are comfortable with the role being unpaid today.
5. They understand that future payment is not guaranteed.
6. They will treat students, colleges, partners and other volunteers respectfully.

Tell candidates not to join if they need guaranteed immediate income or are being pressured by the founder, an ambassador or a friend.

## 15. What the founder must do every month

The contributor offer is real only if the founder or a delegated owner:

- runs onboarding;
- assigns work with a deadline;
- provides the required templates or access;
- checks in weekly or fortnightly;
- removes blockers;
- records contribution;
- gives feedback;
- recognises actual work;
- pays approved reimbursements when promised;
- allows a clean exit;
- publishes what opportunities are real and what are not.

If the team cannot do these things, reduce recruitment instead of recruiting more people.

## 16. Final standard

> **Do not recruit people into an ambition. Recruit them into a clear role with a clear return.**

For Clstr, the return is not currently cash. It is **ownership, evidence, experience, network, learning, recognition and progression consideration**. Those benefits must be delivered through actual work within the first 30 days. If Clstr cannot deliver them, the unpaid model is not fair and should be stopped.
