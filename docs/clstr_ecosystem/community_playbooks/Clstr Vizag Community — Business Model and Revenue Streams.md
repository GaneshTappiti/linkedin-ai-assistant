# Clstr Vizag Community — Business Model and Revenue Streams

**Scope:** The Clstr Vizag student community at [vizag.clstr.in](https://vizag.clstr.in/).

> **Your college gets you a degree. Clstr gets you a city.**

## 1. The business in one sentence

**Clstr Vizag builds a trusted cross-college student community, creates recurring student experiences across social, cultural, creative, collaborative and opportunity formats, and earns revenue from relevant partners that want to support or participate in those experiences.**

Clstr is the community and therefore the product. `clstr.in` is the supporting layer for identity, discovery, registration, participation history, continuity and measurement. It is not the business being sold as a standalone SaaS product.

## 2. What Clstr is actually selling

Clstr is not selling a student database, WhatsApp access, raw impressions or a promise that thousands of students will attend. It sells a **defined student experience** and the operational capability to deliver it safely, measurably and repeatedly.

| Clstr asset | Student value | Partner value |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-college community | Students meet people beyond their own campus. | Relevant access to a citywide student audience, subject to consent and event fit. |
| Circles and Guilds | Students belong around interests or build with others. | A more relevant context than undifferentiated advertising. |
| Events and programs | Students participate in memorable experiences, competitions, showcases, town halls and collaborations. | A defined sponsorship or activation opportunity with a clear audience and deliverable. |
| Campus nodes | Students hear about Clstr through trusted peers and local campus intelligence improves. | Better campus-level distribution than one central broadcast, if node activity is verified. |
| `clstr.in` layer | Identity, discovery, continuity and follow-up do not disappear after one event. | Aggregate, event-level reporting and permission-safe opted-in actions. |
| Contribution and recognition system | Core Team members and Campus Ambassadors build documented proof of work. | More reliable local execution and better student experience. |

The commercial rule is simple: **student value comes first; partner value is built around it.** If a sponsor request damages trust, privacy, safety or the student experience, Clstr should reject the money.

## 3. Customer segments

### Students: the primary users

Clstr should serve students who want belonging, new friendships, collaborators, creative opportunities, city experiences, recognition, mentorship or access beyond their college. These are user hypotheses until validated through interviews, attendance, repeat behavior and meaningful activity.

### Partners: the paying customers

The first commercial targets should be organisations that can see local value without requiring national-scale reach:

| Priority | Paying customer | Why they may pay | Suitable Clstr inventory |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cafés, restaurants, venues and local lifestyle brands | Relevant student footfall, association and content. | Hosted meetups, creator nights, sampling, venue support and category partnership. |
| 2 | Technology companies, startups and coworking spaces | Access to relevant builders, creators and early-career students. | Build Sprints, Guild programs, creator events and career sessions. |
| 3 | Employers, education providers and training organisations | Specific opted-in interaction with students. | Career Connect, information sessions, workshops and application flows. |
| 4 | Colleges, institutions, civic bodies, NGOs and CSR teams | Structured youth participation in approved programs. | Town halls, city challenges, volunteering, cultural and student programs. |
| 5 | Larger regional, national or overseas companies | Brand association and qualified student participation. | Only after Clstr has local case studies, reliable delivery and reporting. |

Colleges and student clubs can be hosts, co-organisers, distribution partners or permission-giving stakeholders. An informal Campus Ambassador relationship is not institutional approval.

## 4. Business model architecture

The model has five connected layers.

### Layer 1 — Free or near-free student access

Students should generally join the community without paying. A small ticket may be used only when a specific experience has genuine direct costs or premium value. Student pricing must be transparent and never disguise sponsor economics.

### Layer 2 — Community programming

Clstr creates repeatable experiences under one student-first identity. Possible formats include social hangouts, Circles, Guild mixers, creator showcases, sports and culture programs, city activities, town halls, competitions, Build Sprints, Career Connect and education sessions.

The community can be broad in event format but must be narrow in identity: **a student in Vizag finding belonging, people and opportunities beyond one campus.**

### Layer 3 — Campus distribution

Campus Captains and Campus Node Coordinators connect Clstr to colleges. They are measured by verified meaningful participation, repeat activity, feedback quality, conduct and node health—not by poster forwarding, WhatsApp group size or raw signups.

### Layer 4 — Partner-funded experiences

A partner funds or supports a clearly defined experience. Clstr specifies the audience, deliverables, safety responsibilities, budget, reporting and consent boundaries before accepting the commitment.

### Layer 5 — Reinvestment and compounding

Collected cash first covers taxes, refunds, contractual obligations and safe delivery. Surplus then funds reserves, moderation, safety, reliability, measurement, student programming, fair contributor compensation where sustainable and only later expansion.

## 5. Revenue streams

### Stream A — In-kind support

A venue, café, printer, food provider, equipment supplier, photographer or media partner covers a defined cost in return for agreed category credit and visibility. In-kind support is useful in the early proof stage, but it must be recorded separately from cash revenue.

### Stream B — Single-event sponsorship

A relevant brand pays for defined association with one Clstr event or program. The package may include approved branding, content, a physical activation, sampling, a speaker slot, prizes or a post-event report. The partner is buying the agreed experience, not an unlimited audience.

### Stream C — Sponsored competitions and challenges

A company funds a creator competition, build challenge, cultural competition, sports activity or city challenge. Clstr provides the participant journey, campus distribution, rules, judging process, safety controls, prizes and results report.

### Stream D — Multi-event community partnership

After Clstr has delivered paid pilots and earned renewal, a partner may fund a defined multi-event program. The agreement must specify the number and type of experiences, inventory, geographic or campus scope, reporting, payment schedule, cancellation rights and delivery owner.

### Stream E — Career and education partnerships

An employer, startup, education provider or training organisation may pay for a specific session, booth, workshop or opted-in opportunity flow. The student must understand the purpose of the interaction and separately consent to any application or contact process. Clstr must never sell a general student contact list.

### Stream F — Institutional, civic and CSR programs

Colleges, institutions, NGOs, civic bodies and CSR teams may fund approved programs such as town halls, youth forums, volunteering, city challenges, cultural initiatives or student development activities. These require written scope, permissions, safety ownership and outcome reporting.

### Stream G — Transparent student tickets

Ticket revenue can fund a specific higher-cost event when the student receives clear value and the price remains accessible. Ticket sales should supplement, not replace, the community’s free-access promise where possible.

### Streams Clstr should not use

Clstr should not sell general student contact lists, charge sponsors for unverified “leads,” pay Campus Ambassadors per contact, promise guaranteed attendance, inflate impressions, mix in-kind support with cash, or make revenue dependent on one sponsor.

## 6. Package architecture

Use stable package names but treat prices as testable commercial anchors, not validated market facts.

| Package | Initial pilot price anchor | What is included | Do not promise |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **In-kind Partner** | Cost coverage only | One defined cost plus agreed category credit. | Cash-equivalent value without recording the basis. |
| **Associate Partner** | ₹25,000–₹50,000 | Defined event association, selected digital assets, recap mention and modest activation. | Guaranteed attendance or unlimited content. |
| **Experience Partner** | ₹50,000–₹1,50,000 | Larger event association, approved activation, content inventory, student experience support and impact report. | “Access to all Vizag students.” |
| **Sponsored Challenge** | ₹75,000–₹2,50,000 | Competition design, campus distribution, judging, prizes, participant management and results report. | Unverified conversions or employment outcomes. |
| **Community Partner** | Custom; only after renewal proof | Defined multi-event program, selected inventory, reporting and review meetings. | Annual exclusivity without capacity and risk controls. |
| **Career/Education Program** | Custom per approved scope | Specific event or opted-in flow with consent, partner verification and reporting. | General database access or unsolicited student contact. |

These are **internal starting anchors for negotiation**, not claims about what the Vizag market will pay. The team must record every proposal, price, objection, close, cash collection, delivery cost, partner satisfaction result and renewal outcome. After at least three comparable proposals or pilots, replace anchors with Clstr’s own observed conversion and margin data.

## 7. What a sponsor proposal must contain

Every proposal should state the event or program, student segment, expected but non-guaranteed participation range, participating colleges if known, partner deliverables, Clstr deliverables, activation rules, consent boundaries, safety responsibilities, production requirements, budget, payment schedule, cancellation terms, reporting format and named owners.

The proposal should use language such as **“target audience”** and **“planned deliverables,”** not “guaranteed reach.” The post-event report should show what actually happened: verified registrations, check-ins, college mix, participation in the activation, opted-in actions, content delivered, satisfaction, incidents and cost variance.

## 8. Unit economics for every event

No event should be judged only by crowd size. Before approval, prepare a budget. After delivery, prepare actuals.

| Metric | Formula or definition |
|---|---|
| Cash event revenue | Cash collected for the event, net of refunds and reversals. |
| Direct event cost | Venue + production + food + transport + security + permits + staff + equipment + prizes + payment fees + refunds. |
| Event contribution margin | Cash event revenue − direct event cost. |
| Fully loaded event result | Contribution margin − allocated operating cost − documented founder time. |
| Cost per meaningful participant | Direct acquisition and delivery cost ÷ verified participants who completed the defined meaningful action. |
| Repeat attendance rate | Returning attendees ÷ eligible attendees, with ambassador or founder chasing separately identified. |
| Partner renewal rate | Partners that pay for another defined objective ÷ partners whose pilots completed. |
| Sponsor concentration | Revenue from largest partner ÷ total cash revenue. |

An event may lose money as an approved learning pilot. It must be labelled as such, have a learning objective and a maximum downside. Repeating a loss without learning is not growth.

## 9. Revenue sequence

The correct order is:

1. Prove that students return without constant personal chasing.
2. Build meaningful Circles, Guilds and post-event activity.
3. Use in-kind support only when it improves delivery without damaging trust.
4. Sell one paid pilot with one relevant partner and a narrow scope.
5. Deliver safely and issue an honest report.
6. Ask for renewal based on delivered value.
7. Offer multi-event partnerships only after repeatable delivery and economics.
8. Consider founder compensation only after sustained surplus, reserves, transparent accounts and routine operations that do not depend entirely on the founder.

## 10. Proof gates

| Gate | Minimum evidence before advancing |
|---|---|
| **Community proof** | Three or four consecutive experiences with repeat or organic invitation behavior. |
| **Product continuity proof** | Students use `clstr.in` between events for discovery, Circles, Guilds, connections, collaboration or a meaningful next action. |
| **Paid pilot proof** | A partner signs a defined scope, pays cash and accepts the delivered report. |
| **Economic proof** | Mature events have positive or deliberately controlled contribution margin after direct costs. |
| **Renewal proof** | At least one partner pays again because Clstr delivered relevant value. |
| **Operating proof** | The five Core Team departments function with two capable people per department, documented handoffs and reduced founder dependence. |
| **Safety and trust proof** | Code of conduct, reporting route, moderation, age/consent controls, event safety plan and permission records work in practice. |
| **Expansion proof** | A trained local operator can reproduce the Vizag operating system without weakening safety, trust or economics. |

## 11. Core Team and contributor economics

The Core Team is not a hidden cost-free labour assumption. The required structure is five departments with two people each: **Logistics; Design + Social; Outreach; Tech + Web; Finance + Ticketing.** Both people in each department must be able to run the work independently.

Until the community has sustainable surplus, contributor roles are unpaid and must be presented honestly. The value exchange is defined ownership, training, feedback, portfolio proof, cross-college network, contribution record, certificate, truthful recommendation-letter eligibility, recognition and progression consideration. The Campus Ambassador reward order remains:

> **M01 Certificate → M02 Recommendation Letter → M03 Merch → M04 Tee/VIP Passes/Recognition.**

These are rewards, not salary. Any future paid task, reimbursement, bonus or revenue share requires an actual funded scope and written terms before work begins.

## 12. Management dashboard

The founder and department owners should review one dashboard every month:

| Area | Minimum measures |
|---|---|
| Student health | Verified members, meaningful activation, repeat attendance, 30/60/90-day activity and organic referrals. |
| Cross-college value | College mix, cross-college connections, collaborations and participation outside the home campus. |
| `clstr.in` usage | Profiles completed, discovery actions, Circle/Guild activity, post-event actions and returning users between events. |
| Campus nodes | Active nodes, verified participation, repeat activity, reporting quality, backup leads and node cost. |
| Event economics | Budget variance, direct cost, contribution margin, refunds, incidents and founder hours. |
| Partner economics | Proposals, cash contracted, cash collected, deliverable completion, satisfaction and renewals. |
| Trust and safety | Complaints, reports, response time, unresolved cases, age/consent issues and permission exceptions. |

## 13. The strategic answer

Clstr Vizag should not try to become a generic advertising agency, a ticketing website, a career portal, a collection of WhatsApp groups or an event company that happens to have an app.

It should become **Vizag’s cross-college student social-discovery community**. Events are the physical expression of the network. Campus nodes are the distribution mechanism. `clstr.in` gives the community memory, continuity and measurement. Partner revenue funds better student experiences, but partner access must remain relevant, permission-safe and subordinate to student trust.

The business works only if this chain becomes visible in evidence:

> **Students join → students find people and belonging → students return → `clstr.in` is useful between events → partners see relevant delivered value → partners pay again → surplus funds safer, better and more repeatable community experiences.**
