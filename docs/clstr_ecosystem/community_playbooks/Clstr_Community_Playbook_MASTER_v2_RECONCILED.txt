01. Identity
Platform motto: “Your college gets you a degree. Clstr gets you a city.”
Community motto (event/energy-facing): “Find your team. Vizag is the campus.”
What we are: A verified, cross-college student community for Visakhapatnam, built on two equal pillars — Collaborate (find people who complement your skills) and Socialize (meet people outside your own campus). Neither pillar works alone: collab-only reads as a job fair, social-only reads as a party scene with no reason to return. We are not a club, not a WhatsApp group, not a placement agency, and not a media company — we are building a persistent, cross-college, real-identity student social-discovery community for people who want to belong, build and grow beyond one campus. Clstr is the community; clstr.in is the supporting layer for identity, discovery, continuity and measurement.
Sequencing: Vizag first, proven small before scaled — then Andhra Pradesh, then India. No step is skipped for speed.

03. Who We’re Actually Building For
Grounded in direct research into real Vizag student behavior (Reddit/Quora threads, hostel policy pages, college review sites, local youth-culture reporting) — not abstract personas.
Collab-hungry (~35 of 100): trapped coders with no designer, invisible designers doing uncredited free work, creators burning out solo, business-minded students with no proof of execution, restless club-veteran seniors wanting a bigger stage, side-hustlers already running small businesses (thrift, baking, content, freelance design, even stock trading) with no peer group who understands it.
Socially-hungry (~45 of 100): anxious freshers navigating real curfew and permission restrictions, quietly isolated commuters, one-good-friend-group students with no reason to cross campuses, outstation/non-local students facing real, documented adjustment difficulty, women navigating hostel curfews and safety concerns at male-skewed engineering campuses, sports/fitness students wanting mixed-college games.
Career/skeptical (~20 of 100): placement-panicked final-years with no reachable alumni, and genuine skeptics burned by dead WhatsApp groups and abandoned college clubs who need proof of life, not promises.
The design rule this creates: every phase, every event type, and every piece of content must serve at least one of these three groups deliberately — never assume growth in one automatically serves the others.

04. Two Tracks: Guilds and Circles
Every member picks 1-2 at signup — not exclusive, many belong to both.
Guilds (skill/collab-oriented): Build (coders, engineers, product-minded), Design (graphic design, UI/UX, video editing, photography), Create (content creators, writers, podcasters), Hustle (business, marketing, side-hustlers), Grow (career-focused, mentorship-seeking — the on-ramp for undecided students).
Circles (pure social/interest, zero performance bar): Foodies, Music, Gaming, Fitness & Sports, Travel & Explore, Beach Crew, Photography-for-fun, Movies/Anime, Book Club, New to Vizag (specifically for outstation/non-local students facing real adjustment difficulty).
Tracked monthly: Guild/Circle balance across total membership. If signups skew heavily Guild-only, the community is drifting collab-only in practice regardless of what this document says — this must be caught early, not discovered at Month 6.

05. The Hard Logistics Reality (Curfews, Permissions, Cost)
Real research finding, not assumption: hostel curfews are severe and vary widely — GITAM enforces 9:00 PM weekday / 9:30 PM weekend return with biometric attendance; several other colleges enforce 6:00-7:00 PM curfews, especially for women and freshers; first-year students at some hostels may only leave campus once or twice a month, sometimes requiring a parent or guardian. Written warden permission is commonly required for any outing. Sunday/holiday outings (roughly 9am-6pm) are explicitly the most permitted window across multiple colleges. Fee and cost sensitivity is real and frequently named even at well-regarded colleges.
This directly shapes event design — not a detail, a structural requirement:
    • Run two parallel timing tracks, not one: a late-afternoon/early-evening window (4:00-6:30 PM) for smaller Circle/Guild touchpoints that need to include freshers and hostel-restricted students, and a Sunday daytime window (10 AM-5 PM) for anything bigger — this is the actual open door for your most restricted members, not a weekday evening.
    • Provide a simple, official-looking event confirmation flyer (date, time, exact venue, Clstr contact) that a student can show their warden or parents — solves a real, named permission barrier directly.
    • Keep pricing near-zero (₹0-99) through at least Phase 2 — cost sensitivity is real, not just a startup-budget constraint.
    • Encourage and normalize group arrival/departure at events, matching the “go out in groups for safety” norm already embedded in how students are raised to think about outings.

06. Phase 0 — Start Now (Weeks 1-6, informal, no app, no announcements)
We validate the core idea with real people before building any machinery.
Week 1: Founder + 3-5 known contacts each bring 2-3 people from different colleges — no two people from the same college unless unavoidable. Deliberately mix archetypes across all three segments (Section 03), not just builders. Target: 20-30 people, 5-6 colleges. One WhatsApp group with a name that has energy, not a corporate-sounding one.
Weeks 2-6 (biweekly small meetups): Cafe, terrace, park, or free campus common area. Near-zero cost, no tickets, no venue booking. 15-minute structured opener (name, college, one thing you’re into, one thing you’re looking for) doubles as informal Guild/Circle data, then fully open. Rotate venue by college from meetup #2 onward to avoid an early “this is a Raghu thing” perception.
Gate to Phase 1: A behavior gate, not a user-count gate. Move forward once the small group is self-sustaining — people invite friends unprompted, attendance holds or grows without heavy reminding, across 3-4 consecutive meetups. Typically Week 6-8.

07. Phase 1 — Founding Cohort & Ambassador Recruitment (Month 2-3)
    • Identify 1-2 natural connectors per represented college from the Phase 0 group as first Campus Captain candidates. Explicitly seek Guild/Circle diversity per college — don’t default only to “most club/fest experience,” since that’s the exact profile GDG, E-Cell, and TiE are already recruiting.
    • Formalize WhatsApp-to-platform migration once the signup/verification flow is ready.
    • Begin institutional relationship building in parallel — Campus Ops Lead meets each target college’s TPO/student union/cultural committee, working toward a lightweight MOU or written understanding, not just a student-to-student handshake. This is the piece Student Tribe’s field model has that ours was missing, and it protects against a college administration blocking activity later.
    • Founding Night: small, invite-only, ambassador network only (60-80 people), Sunday daytime or early-evening depending on attendee mix. Neutral or rotating venue.
    • Begin light pre-launch buzz mechanics (Section 09) — still no public community announcement.

09. Buzz & Scarcity Mechanics
Buzz is engineered, not announced. Runs underneath Phases 1-2 onward.
    • Unbranded teaser phase: posters/stickers with just a symbol and the community motto, no app name, 2-3 weeks before any public event.
    • Public running counter: a visible signup number (“[LIVE VERIFIED MEMBER COUNT — UPDATE ONLY FROM THE KPI DASHBOARD]. [COLLEGES LEFT — UPDATE ONLY FROM VERIFIED DATA]”).
    • Permanent Founding Cohort badge on early members’ profiles — free, and a real reason for Phase 1 ambassadors to push hard before “founding” status closes.
    • Capped events, not maximized ones — a visibly sold-out or waitlisted event generates more word-of-mouth than a comfortably-attended one.
    • One signature, shareable moment per phase, not five mediocre ones — a mass cross-college photo, a public leaderboard of which college has the most members, a stunt at an existing fest.
    • Distributed content: each active Campus Captain runs or feeds a campus-specific handle (“Clstr @ GITAM”), reposted centrally — multiplies reach per college instead of bottlenecking one account. Raw, phone-shot content in the same aesthetic register students already consume (soft-flex, beach-edit style reels) outperforms polished, corporate-style event photography for this audience.
    • One simple buzz message per phase — Months 1-3: “the city’s students, finally in one place.” Months 4-6: shift to showcasing real collab and social wins as proof. Don’t say everything at once; it dilutes what actually spreads by word of mouth.
    • Authentic content hook: a “Vizag Student Hustles” spotlight series profiling real students already running side businesses — grounded in real, evidenced behavior, not manufactured hype, and seeds the Hustle Guild with real people from day one.

10. Phase 2 — Soft Public Launch (Month 3-4)
    • First public event: Campus Takeover format (see Events Plan), free or near-free, Sunday daytime, no collab-structure — let it breathe. Venue rotates away from Founding Night’s location.
    • Public signup opens city-wide, still framed as “earned access” via the running counter and Founding badge mechanics.
    • Weekly cadence goes live: rotating Guild micro-prompt, rotating Circle prompt/vibe-check, “Collab of the Week” spotlight (seeded manually by the Community/Collab Lead early on if needed). This is the connective tissue between monthly flagships — without it, the community feels dead in the weeks between events.

11. Phase 3 — Scale Within Vizag (Month 5-6)
    • Full monthly event cadence live across all formats (see Events Plan).
    • Career Connect 01 — only once real signup numbers and 2-3 prior successful smaller events support the pitch to companies. Don’t force this to land exactly on an unproven milestone.
    • Community/Collab Lead role active since Month 2-3 (not starting cold at Month 4).

Revenue ladder — earn the right to charge: 1. Phase 0-1: No sponsors at all. Prove repeat attendance first — this is the actual asset every future sponsor conversation is built on. 2. Phase 2: In-kind only (a cafe covers snacks for visibility, a print shop does banners for credit) — zero cash changes hands, low-risk proof-of-concept for both sides. 3. Phase 3: First real cash sponsorship, only once there’s a track record (2-3 successful smaller events) and content proof (photos, reels, testimonials). 4. Scale: Community Partner (annual) tier — the big revenue unlock, once a brand can see a full year of consistent, varied events across all three segments.
Hard rule — protects trust, non-negotiable: No per-lead or per-contact payment structures, ever, to any sponsor or partner. Sponsors pay for visibility and room access, never for a list of student contacts to take away. If a company wants direct student contact, that requires a separate, explicit, event-specific opt-in at registration — never bundled into general membership. Local businesses near colleges (MVP, Siripuram, Dwarka Nagar cafes already functioning as informal student hubs) are a realistic, closer-to-home first sponsor category before chasing PSUs or big IT companies.

14. Metrics (Unified — Platform + Event)
    • Verified signups, by college, tracked against the 19-college list
    • Repeat attendance rate — % of attendees at a 2nd+ event who weren’t personally re-invited by an ambassador — the real signal of organic stickiness vs. manufactured turnout
    • Cross-Guild collabs completed (a completed collab is proof; a swapped Instagram handle isn’t)
    • Guild/Circle balance across total membership
    • Gender ratio per event (tracked, not assumed)
    • Institutional MOU status per college
    • Sponsor revenue by segment/tier

16. Expansion: Vizag → Andhra Pradesh → India
Realistic timeline, grounded in how long comparable players actually took — Student Tribe organically over ~8 years, Under25 from 2013 to cultural relevance by 2021, with explosive scale only after a 2024 acquisition by a media conglomerate. Treat Month 6 as “Vizag proven,” not “AP launched.” Honest AP-wide traction timeline is 12-18 months; India-wide is multi-year and likely needs external funding.
The one mechanic to borrow before expanding: license the event format to Campus Captains rather than running everything centrally. A model where one Ops Lead personally runs every event breaks the moment you’re in 3+ cities. Under25’s Summit At Campus pattern — student leaders own sponsorship, production, and promotion locally, HQ provides brand/platform/cross-city promotion only — is the structural pattern to copy once Vizag is genuinely proven, not before.
