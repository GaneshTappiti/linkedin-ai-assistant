# Clstr Vizag — Investment Committee Killer Questions

**Document type:** Pre-meeting investment-committee diligence interrogation

**Reference date:** 23 August 2026

**Investor stance:** Assume nothing. Accept no market-size claim, traction claim, product claim, sponsor claim or moat claim without evidence.

## Investment posture before the questions

On the current materials, Clstr presents a promising community thesis but not an investable company. The public Vizag page describes Clstr as free to join, verified, present across 30+ colleges, and currently founded around a WhatsApp group with 20–30 founding members. [1] The main platform describes a verified campus network and digital-campus product, but the public page is concise and does not prove the full proposed product surface. [2]

The purpose of this list is not to help the founder produce a better story. It is to determine whether there is a real business, a repeatable growth engine, a defensible product, and a path to investor returns.

## Minimum evidence package before a second meeting

An investor should not schedule a serious second meeting without receiving:

1. Legal-entity and cap-table information.
2. Twelve months of bank statements and monthly P&L, even if revenue is zero.
3. Student funnel from invite to meaningful retained activity, by college and source.
4. Event register with registrations, check-ins, costs, incidents, satisfaction and repeat attendance.
5. Sponsor pipeline split into paid, contracted, verbal, proposed and rejected.
6. At least one fully reported paid or in-kind pilot with actual partner feedback.
7. Live product walkthrough and architecture/security summary.
8. Data inventory, privacy notice, consent flow, retention policy and minors policy.
9. Campus-node performance report with active definition and succession plan.
10. Twelve-month use-of-funds plan tied to measurable gates.

Until these exist, the investor is not evaluating an investable company. The investor is evaluating a founder’s conviction.
