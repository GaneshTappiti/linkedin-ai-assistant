# Clstr Vizag Student Community — Canonical Baseline

**Status:** Canonical community source of truth

**Version:** 1.0 — reconciled August 2026

**Scope:** This document is only about the Clstr Vizag student community at `vizag.clstr.in`. `clstr.in` is the supporting technology layer; it is not the subject of this document as a separate platform business.

## 1. Identity

Clstr is building a **cross-college student social-discovery community for Vizag**. It helps students belong, discover people, participate in student life, collaborate, create, perform and grow beyond their own campus.

> **Your college gets you a degree. Clstr gets you a city.**

> **Find your team. Vizag is the campus.**

The community is the product. Real student relationships, participation, memories, opportunities and citywide belonging are the value. clstr.in supports the community with identity, discovery, verification where available, event registration, continuity, contribution history, connections and measurement.

Clstr is not a claim that other communities are unimportant or nonexistent. Official institutional pages show extensive student clubs and activity inside major Vizag campuses, while city directories show active technology, founder, hobby and other groups. Coding groups, entrepreneurship communities, volunteer organisations, running clubs, college clubs, career pages and event operators can all be valuable partners or adjacent alternatives. Clstr’s intended category is broader: **the student layer connecting life across Vizag colleges**.

## 2. Public positioning

### Approved short description

**Clstr is Vizag’s student community. Meet people beyond your college, find your circles, build with your city and grow together.**

### Product-supported description

**Clstr connects students across Vizag through real community and events. clstr.in gives that community a persistent home for identity, discovery, participation and connections.**

### Approved differentiation

No single feature is unique in isolation. Clstr’s potential advantage is the combination of:

| Element | Community meaning |
|---|---|
| Student identity | The common anchor is being a student in Vizag, not only being a coder, founder, runner, volunteer or member of one campus. |
| Cross-college reach | Students can discover people and experiences beyond their own institution. |
| Social discovery | Belonging and low-pressure participation are primary value, not an afterthought to jobs or skills. |
| Broad programming | Music, culture, creators, sport, gaming, competitions, town halls, careers, education, civic activity and collaboration can fit the same student identity. |
| Campus nodes | Trusted students provide local distribution, context and succession. |
| clstr.in continuity | The community does not reset after one event, one chat group or one student leader graduates. |
| Trusted partner access | Partners receive relevant visibility and aggregate reporting, not a general student contact list. |

### Language to avoid

Do not say that Clstr is literally the only student community in Vizag, that no alternative exists, that every competitor is only a WhatsApp group, that the market contains a confirmed number of students without a source, that an event will definitely attract 500–5,000 people, that a sponsor has agreed until cash is contracted, or that a college/TPO has granted official access without written permission.

If “first” is needed for a campaign, qualify it narrowly and honestly: **“building Vizag’s cross-college student social-discovery community”** is safer than an unqualified “first student community.” The evidence does not support saying that no student clubs or student communities exist in Vizag.

## 3. Community architecture

Clstr has two participation tracks.

**Circles** are interest and belonging spaces with no performance requirement. Examples include food, music, gaming, sport, travel, beach activities, movies, books, photography and “new to Vizag.”

**Guilds** are skill and collaboration spaces. Examples include build, design, create, hustle and grow/career.

Members can participate in both. The community must track whether Circles are genuinely active or whether the brand is drifting into a career or collaboration club. Every month should include at least one low-pressure social action and one collaboration-oriented action when readiness permits.

## 4. Student promise

Clstr’s student promise is expressed through three verbs:

| Promise | Meaning | Example proof |
|---|---|---|
| **Belong** | Meet students, make friends and participate without needing status or performance. | Circles, hangouts, Campus Takeovers, social and cultural events. |
| **Build** | Find complementary people and create something real. | Guilds, CollabHub, Build Sprints, creator collaborations and showcases. |
| **Grow** | Gain useful connections, recognition and opportunity access. | Mentorship, Career Connect, education events, contribution records and honest outcomes. |

The event domain can change. The student promise cannot.

## 5. Campus distribution

The campus system is not a list of ambassadors. It is a network of active, measurable and replaceable campus nodes.

**Campus Captain** is an ambassador-program role responsible for activation at one college during a defined sprint. They recruit, orient, support event participation, report blockers and contribute local context.

**Campus Node Coordinator** is an ongoing community role promoted from a proven Campus Captain. The Coordinator maintains the college presence, supports community participation, identifies successors and reports campus pulse. The Ambassador Program Manager and Community/Collab Lead must remain distinct owners unless the team explicitly combines those duties in writing.

Each active node should have a primary lead, a backup, a defined college relationship status, relevant Guild/Circle representation, a succession plan and a monthly report. Appointed ambassadors are not counted as reach. A node is active only when it produces verified meaningful participation, repeat activity, useful feedback or event contribution.

## 6. Events

Events are the physical expression of the community, not the entire community. The 12-month calendar is a target sequence, not a rigid promise. Each event must pass a readiness gate.

Before announcement, the owner must document:

- purpose and target student segment;
- venue and capacity;
- permissions and institutional relationship status;
- timing and transport/curfew considerations;
- age eligibility and safeguarding;
- code of conduct and reporting route;
- emergency and first-aid plan;
- budget, contingency and funding fallback;
- registration, check-in and refund process;
- photography/content consent;
- sponsor deliverables, if any;
- clstr.in workflow and post-event follow-up.

The first operating sequence should prioritise small founding meetups, a low-cost cross-college social event, a Guild/Circle activity, a creator or culture format, and only later company-facing or large flagship events. Events should be scheduled in permission-friendly windows after checking the actual target colleges rather than assuming one curfew rule applies everywhere.

Event success requires more than attendance. Track check-ins, first-time versus returning students, college mix, Guild/Circle mix, cross-college connections, satisfaction, incidents, content consent, sponsor deliverables and event contribution margin.

## 7. Revenue model

Students should have free or low-barrier access while Clstr builds trust and density. Partners may pay for relevant, measurable experiences—not for a general database of student contacts.

The commercial sequence is:

1. **Founding proof:** no sponsor dependency; prove students return.
2. **Early public proof:** in-kind support only where it improves the experience.
3. **Paid pilot:** negotiated event sponsorship after documented participation and delivery evidence.
4. **Repeat partner:** renew a paid partner based on an honest impact report.
5. **Annual partner:** offer multi-event association only after Clstr can deliver and report a full program.

Possible partner value includes event association, activation, content, sampling, competitions, cultural programming, creator showcases, career sessions, education sessions, city challenges and aggregate impact reporting. Pricing remains a planning hypothesis until actual paid pilots and renewals validate willingness to pay.

**Non-negotiable trust rule:** Clstr does not sell a general student contact list. Any job, education, interview or direct opportunity flow must be purpose-specific, separately explained and explicitly opted into by the student. Partners receive only the data and access they are authorised to receive.

Revenue is allocated first to taxes/obligations, safe event delivery, reserves, product reliability and security, community operations, fair contributor compensation and only then expansion experiments.

## 8. Team model

The community uses two layers.

**Layer 1 — Core Operations:** Community/Collab, Events/Logistics, Brand/Content and Partnerships/Sponsorship. These roles own work that breaks if nobody shows up.

**Layer 2 — Community Volunteers:** Guild/Circle Leads, Campus Node Coordinators and Community Hype/Content Creators. These roles extend reach and participation but should not be the sole owner of safety-critical, financial or contractual work.

While the community is unfunded, no fixed salary, stipend, bonus or guaranteed future payment should be promised. Recognition, access and portfolio credit must be described honestly and must not be presented as equivalent to employment or cash compensation. Future pay or revenue share requires actual surplus, written terms, appropriate records and team approval.

The founder retains vision, major relationships, hiring decisions, major sponsor decisions, formal public representation and high-risk financial approvals. Routine member management, content, event execution, campus support and reporting must move to named owners. The founder should test a two-week absence from daily operations before expansion.

## 9. KPI dictionary

The single dashboard must distinguish:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Verified member | A person who completed the currently approved identity/verification flow. |
| Activated member | A verified member who completed a meaningful action beyond signup. |
| Meaningfully active member | A member who participates in a defined community action during the measurement period. |
| Returning member | A member who participates again in a later period or event. |
| Active node | A college node producing verified meaningful participation and reporting, not merely holding a title. |
| Repeat attendance | The share of attendees who return to another event, with ambassador-personal-chasing separately identified. |
| Completed collaboration | A real cross-college or cross-Guild output, not an exchanged handle or intention. |
| Sponsor revenue | Cash contracted and collected, net of refunds or reversals; verbal interest is pipeline. |
| In-kind value | A separately recorded estimate of goods/services received; never mixed with cash revenue. |
| Event contribution margin | Cash event revenue minus directly attributable event delivery costs, with founder time and in-kind value shown separately. |

Headline dashboards should prioritise meaningful activity, retention, repeat attendance, event economics, partner renewal, safety and founder dependence over raw signups, follower counts, QR scans, number of ambassadors or number of events.

## 10. Evidence discipline

The following remain hypotheses until Clstr produces a reproducible source: total Vizag student population, total institutions, number of engineering students, 19-college coverage, 500–5,000 event demand, 2,000 students per college, 10+ monthly events, sponsor willingness to pay, placement outcomes, official TPO access and literal first/only status.

The official district profile and AISHE directory are useful context but do not by themselves establish a complete citywide student census. Official IIM Visakhapatnam and GITAM Visakhapatnam pages confirm substantial existing campus-club ecosystems, and Meetup confirms active city-level groups; neither establishes Clstr’s demand or a complete citywide student census. Public competitor figures are treated as company claims unless independently verified.

## 11. Stage gates

| Gate | Required before moving forward |
|---|---|
| Founding proof | Students attend repeatedly, invite others and participate without constant founder chasing. |
| Public launch | A small cross-college event can be run safely, affordably and with post-event follow-up. |
| Community engine | Students use Circles/Guilds or other community spaces between events; active nodes and backups exist. |
| Paid partner pilot | A partner pays cash for a defined event objective and receives accurate reporting. |
| Repeatability | Multiple formats work with positive or controlled contribution margin, retention and documented operations. |
| Expansion | A trained non-founder operator can reproduce the community system in another area without weakening trust or safety. |

## 12. Canonical conclusion

Clstr should not be built as a collection of impressive events. It should be built as a durable student community in which events create human connection, campus nodes create local trust, clstr.in preserves continuity, and partners fund experiences without extracting student trust.

The operating order is:

> **Student value → repeated participation → cross-college density → measurable partner value → surplus → reinvestment → controlled expansion.**

Any plan that reverses this order is premature.
