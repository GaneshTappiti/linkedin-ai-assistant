# Clstr Vizag — Core Team & Campus Ambassador Value Proposition

**Version:** 1.0

**Date:** 26 August 2026

**Audience:** Potential Core Team members, Campus Ambassadors and Campus Node Coordinators.

**Scope:** This document is only about the Clstr Vizag student community. Clstr is the community. `clstr.in` is the supporting layer that helps the community connect, discover, register, continue and measure participation.

## 1. The problem with the current pitch

The current pitch asks people to join a large ambition but does not clearly explain the exchange. “We are building something huge” is not a benefit. “You will get exposure” is not a benefit unless exposure is defined. “You may receive future opportunities” is not a benefit unless the conditions are written and realistic.

An unpaid role can be ethical and worthwhile only when the person receives clear value during the role, understands the workload, has real authority, builds credible evidence of contribution and is free to leave without pressure. Clstr must not recruit unpaid people with vague promises of money, jobs, equity, certificates, fame or guaranteed access.

## 2. The Clstr mission and motto

### Recommended public motto

> **Your college gets you a degree. Clstr gets you a city.**

This is short, memorable and explains why the community exists. It does not claim that Clstr replaces a college, club or specialist community.

### Supporting line

> **Find your team. Vizag is the campus.**

### Community promise

> **Clstr helps students belong, build and grow beyond their own campus.**

### Full explanation

Clstr is building Vizag’s cross-college student social-discovery community. It creates spaces where students can meet people beyond their college, find their circles, collaborate across skills, participate in culture and student life, discover useful opportunities and create memories together. `clstr.in` gives this community a persistent layer for identity, discovery, event participation, connections and continuity.

## 3. The value exchange

The honest exchange is:

| They contribute | Clstr must provide |
|---|---|
| Time and consistent execution | A defined role, manageable workload and a named decision owner. |
| Campus access and local context | Real ownership of a campus or function—not just a promotional title. |
| Recruitment and communication | Training, playbooks, templates, feedback and a functioning community to invite people into. |
| Event support | Practical experience in planning, operations, communication and leadership. |
| Creativity and initiative | A place to ship work that can be shown as a credible portfolio artifact. |
| Trust and responsibility | Honest recognition, contribution records and transparent performance feedback. |
| Learning and experimentation | Access to cross-college peers, mentors, organisers, creators and selected partner sessions where available. |

If Clstr cannot provide the right-hand side, it should not ask for the left-hand side.

## 4. What the person receives immediately

### A. Real ownership

A selected person receives a defined operating area, not a decorative designation. A Core Team lead may own events, community/Collab, brand/content or partnerships. A Campus Captain may own a time-bound activation sprint at one college. A Campus Node Coordinator may own continuity after demonstrating reliable execution.

Ownership means the person can make decisions within a written boundary, access the resources needed for the work, propose improvements and receive credit for outcomes.

### B. A serious portfolio of work

Clstr should help each contributor produce documented evidence such as:

- a campus activation plan and results report;
- an event concept, run sheet, budget and post-event review;
- a community campaign with creative assets and measured participation;
- a cross-college collaboration delivered to completion;
- a sponsor or partner proposal with approved deliverables;
- a Circle or Guild program with participant feedback;
- a safety or operations checklist improved through actual use.

A certificate alone is weak. A specific portfolio artifact with the contributor’s role, scope, outcome and evidence is stronger.

### C. Leadership and professional skills

The role can provide practice in communication, public speaking, stakeholder management, event operations, community moderation, negotiation, content, reporting, conflict handling, prioritisation and team coordination.

Clstr must not claim that participation guarantees a job. The honest promise is that a contributor can build experience and evidence that may strengthen their future applications if they perform well.

### D. Cross-college network

A contributor can meet students, creators, organisers, Guild/Circle leads, college clubs, mentors and approved partners from across Vizag. The value is not a promise that every person will receive a job or investor connection. The value is access to a wider student network that would not exist inside one campus alone.

### E. Visibility and recognition

Recognition includes a verified contribution record and, after successful completion of the defined role period and deliverables, a Clstr completion certificate. A role-specific letter of recommendation may be issued when the work is documented well enough for the recommender to write a truthful letter. Other recognition can include public member spotlights, event credits, role badges and opportunities to represent a project the contributor genuinely delivered.

Recommendations must be earned, specific and signed by an accountable person. A generic certificate with no record of work is not a meaningful benefit; Clstr’s certificate should state the actual role and completed contribution.

### F. Learning access

Clstr can offer internal training sessions on event planning, community building, content, sponsor communication, safety, moderation, public speaking and reporting. Selected experts or partners may contribute sessions when appropriate.

Training must be scheduled and delivered. “Learning opportunities” should not remain a phrase in the recruitment message.

### G. First access to leadership progression

A Campus Captain who consistently delivers can be considered for Campus Node Coordinator, Circle/Guild Lead, Event Lead, Core Team Lead or a future paid project role when one actually exists.

Progression is earned through evidence. It is not automatic and is not a promise of employment.

## 5. What the Core Team receives

The Core Team uses five departments with two people in each department. Both people must be able to run the department independently; there is no “lead and assistant” dependency.

| Department | What it owns |
|---|---|
| Logistics | Events, venue, run-of-show, day-of execution and vendor coordination. |
| Design + Social | Content, brand, Instagram, event creatives and campus-specific handles. |
| Outreach | Sponsors, companies, colleges and ambassador coordination. |
| Tech + Web | `vizag.clstr.in` upkeep, form fixes, CollabHub support and basic troubleshooting. |
| Finance + Ticketing | Event budgets, ticket-sales tracking, sponsor payment records and surplus reporting. |

The Founder retains vision, key relationships, hiring decisions and high-risk approvals. Department pairs own day-to-day execution. Community/Collab and Campus Ops coordinate across departments when needed.

The Core Team is for people who want deeper responsibility and can commit consistently.

| Core Team area | What they own | Concrete value they build |
|---|---|---|
| Community / Collab | Community rhythm, member experience, Circles, Guilds and collaborations. | Community design, moderation, relationship management and outcome tracking. |
| Events / Logistics | Event concept, run sheet, vendors, venue, capacity, safety and post-event review. | End-to-end event operations and execution proof. |
| Brand / Content | Editorial direction, student stories, creator formats, visual identity and distribution. | Portfolio-quality content systems and measurable campaigns. |
| Partnerships / Sponsorship | Partner discovery, proposals, deliverables, reporting and renewal process. | Sales, negotiation, account management and commercial judgment. |
| Campus Operations | Node health, college relationships, permission status, backups and succession. | Distributed operations and stakeholder management. |

The Core Team receives more authority than a Campus Ambassador, but also carries a higher reliability requirement. A core role should not be offered to someone who can only participate occasionally.

## 6. What a Campus Ambassador receives

A Campus Ambassador/Campus Captain is a **90-day sprint** for one college or a small approved campus team. The numerical targets below are internal campaign targets, not verified market facts; they must be confirmed before each campaign and measured using verified participation.

| Milestone | Internal target | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **M01** | 500 verified students and 2 active clubs or campus communities. | Completion certificate. |
| **M02** | 1,000 verified students and 4 active clubs or campus communities. | Role-specific recommendation letter when documented work supports a truthful letter. |
| **M03** | 1,500 verified students and all agreed domain clubs/spaces active. | Clstr merchandise, such as a cap or hoodie, subject to stock. |
| **M04** | 2,000 verified students and full campus-plan activation. | Clstr tee, free VIP passes to eligible events and recognition, subject to capacity and published rules. |
| **M05** | Top performer at semester end, judged on quality, retention, reporting, conduct and community outcome—not raw numbers alone. | Fast-track review for Campus Node Coordinator, Core Team consideration or a funded paid opportunity if one exists; founder appreciation letter and case-study feature with consent. |

The sequence is **certificate → recommendation letter → merchandise → tee/VIP passes/recognition → progression and paid-opportunity consideration**. These rewards are not salary. Spam, fake signups, purchased accounts, unsafe activity and unauthorised college representation do not qualify.

A Campus Ambassador is a defined activation role, not free advertising labour.

### Role value

- a clear campus-level responsibility during a defined sprint;
- training on how to communicate Clstr accurately;
- practical experience in peer outreach and event activation;
- access to cross-college community activities;
- a verified record of completed contribution;
- a completion certificate after the defined role period and required deliverables are completed;
- a role-specific letter of recommendation when the work is completed successfully and documented;
- recognition based on actual work;
- consideration for Campus Node Coordinator or another leadership role after proof;
- participation in selected ambassador-only learning or feedback sessions;
- the ability to influence how Clstr serves their college.

### What an ambassador does not receive automatically

- salary, stipend or guaranteed cash;
- a guaranteed internship or job;
- guaranteed event entry for every event;
- certificate for merely joining; a Clstr completion certificate is issued after the defined role period and required deliverables are completed;
- guaranteed access to sponsors or senior professionals;
- equity, revenue share or future compensation;
- permission to represent a sponsor or college officially without approval.

If a paid task, travel reimbursement, prize or stipend becomes available, it must be separately announced with written eligibility and payment terms.

## 7. What a Campus Node Coordinator receives

The Coordinator is not simply a promoted ambassador. It is an ongoing community leadership role.

The Coordinator receives:

- a defined college node to maintain;
- access to operating dashboards or reports appropriate to the role;
- authority to recommend campus activities within approved boundaries;
- a backup and succession process rather than single-person dependency;
- regular feedback from Community/Collab and Campus Operations;
- contribution documentation covering continuity and outcomes;
- eligibility for higher responsibility when the community is able to support it.

The Coordinator must not be responsible alone for safety-critical, financial or contractual decisions.

## 8. Benchmark: what credible programs do better

Large student-leader programs do not rely only on ambition. They make the value concrete.

Microsoft describes Student Ambassadors as community builders and provides short, immersive rotations involving real teams, practical skills, professional exposure and stories contributors can use in their resume and LinkedIn profile. It also points students to learning, startup, career and tool resources.[1]

Google describes its developer community leads as gaining hands-on experience, technical and leadership skills, mentorship, a global leader network and the opportunity to create local impact. Its public program description also shows that leads host real events and solve real problems with peers.[2] [3]

Clstr cannot copy the budget or brand power of Microsoft or Google. It can copy the underlying discipline:

| Benchmark lesson | Clstr implementation |
|---|---|
| Real work beats vague exposure | Give every role a defined deliverable and evidence record. |
| Learning must be scheduled | Run a monthly skill or operator session. |
| Leadership needs community | Create a cross-college Core Team and node network. |
| Recognition needs proof | Issue contribution records based on actual outputs. |
| Access must be specific | Offer selected sessions, events or introductions without promising jobs. |
| Progression must be earned | Use stage gates for Captain, Coordinator and Core Team roles. |

## 9. The Clstr contributor contract

Before joining, every person should see a one-page role agreement containing:

- role title and owner;
- mission of the role;
- expected weekly time range;
- start date and review date;
- three to five responsibilities;
- decision rights and escalation route;
- safety and conduct requirements;
- success metrics;
- exact benefits available now;
- what is not promised;
- recognition and contribution-record rules;
- exit process;
- data and confidentiality expectations.

The agreement should say plainly:

> **This is a volunteer leadership and community-building role. It is not employment and does not guarantee salary, stipend, internship, job placement, equity or future payment. Clstr will provide defined ownership, training, community access and feedback. After the defined role period and required deliverables are completed, Clstr will provide a completion certificate; a role-specific letter of recommendation will be provided when the contribution is successfully completed and documented well enough for a truthful letter.**

## 10. What Clstr must build to make the promise real

The value proposition will fail if it remains only in a deck. Clstr must implement:

1. **A role onboarding session** with the mission, boundaries, tools, conduct and first assignment.
2. **A role-specific playbook** for every Core Team and ambassador position.
3. **A 30-day contribution plan** with one useful output, not only signup targets.
4. **A monthly learning session** delivered by a responsible owner.
5. **A contribution ledger** recording role, dates, deliverables, outcomes and feedback.
6. **A monthly recognition system** based on evidence, not popularity alone.
7. **A quarterly progression review** for Captain, Coordinator and Core Team movement.
8. **A safe exit process** that does not shame inactive volunteers.
9. **A written reimbursement or prize policy** if money becomes available.
10. **A founder-independent owner** responsible for contributor experience.

## 11. The first 30 days for a new contributor

| Period | Contributor experience |
|---|---|
| Days 1–3 | Mission, motto, role boundary, code of conduct, tools and named owner. |
| Days 4–7 | Shadow one activity and complete a small first task. |
| Week 2 | Deliver one defined campus, Circle, Guild, content or event-support output. |
| Week 3 | Review results with the owner and improve the process. |
| Week 4 | Publish or record the contribution, receive feedback and choose the next responsibility. |

The person should know within one month whether Clstr is giving them real ownership and whether the role is worth continuing.

## 12. Direct recruitment pitch

### Core Team version

> **Your college gets you a degree. Clstr gets you a city.**
>
> Vizag has talented students, but most student life is still divided by campus. Clstr is building a cross-college student community where people can belong, build and grow together. We are looking for a small Core Team that will not just promote events; it will build the community system itself.
>
> You will own a real function, ship work that you can show in your portfolio, build leadership and operating skills, meet students across Vizag, receive structured feedback and earn recognition based on what you actually deliver. This is currently an unpaid volunteer leadership role. We do not promise salary, jobs, internships, equity or future payment. We do promise clear responsibility, training, access to the community, honest credit and a serious opportunity to build something real.
>
> If you want a title, do not apply. If you want responsibility and evidence of work, apply for one role and commit to the first 30 days.

### Campus Ambassador version

> Become the person who connects your college to the wider student life of Vizag.
>
> As a Clstr Campus Ambassador, you will help students in your college discover a cross-college community, participate in relevant events and find people beyond their normal campus circle. You will receive role training, a defined activation sprint, access to the Clstr community, a contribution record, feedback and consideration for future community leadership based on performance.
>
> This is currently unpaid. There is no guaranteed salary, internship, job, certificate or future payment. We will not ask you to spam people or make claims we cannot prove. We will give you a real role, clear tasks and honest recognition for completed work.

## 13. The answer to “Why should I do this?”

The answer should be direct:

> **Do this if you want to build something real, lead people, create a portfolio of work, meet students across Vizag and shape a community that does not yet have a strong cross-college identity. Do not do this if you are looking only for immediate money, a guaranteed job, a decorative certificate or a title.**

Clstr cannot manufacture a benefit it does not have. The team must earn the right to make stronger promises by delivering training, access, recognition, real projects and—only when financially possible—paid opportunities.

## 14. Recruitment red flags

Clstr should reject its own recruitment message if it:

- uses the student population estimate as a personal benefit;
- says “exposure” without naming the people, work or access involved;
- promises future salary, equity or jobs informally;
- demands unlimited availability from unpaid contributors;
- treats ambassadors as a broadcast workforce;
- pays only for raw contacts or signups;
- issues certificates without evidence of contribution;
- hides the unpaid nature of the role until after selection;
- asks students to represent colleges or sponsors without permission;
- makes contributors pay to receive a role, certificate or opportunity.

## 15. The standard Clstr should aim for

The real promise is not “join our huge future.” It is:

> **Join Clstr to own a real piece of Vizag’s student community, build evidence of leadership, meet people beyond your campus and help create experiences that students genuinely return for.**

That is valuable even before cash compensation exists. It is also honest. If Clstr delivers this consistently, unpaid participation can be a legitimate early-stage leadership exchange. If Clstr does not deliver it, the role is simply unpaid labour and people are right to decline.
