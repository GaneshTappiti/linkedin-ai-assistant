Updated August 2026 - Supersedes v1, reconciled with Playbook v2
Table of Contents

00. What Changed From v1
    • Timeline realigned to the Community Playbook’s actual Phase 0 start (informal, this week) — “Month 1” below now means Month 1 of Phase 1 (ambassador/founding stage), roughly 2 months after real Phase 0 begins, not Month 1 of the whole project.
    • Removed all per-lead payment/data-monetization language. Sponsors and partners pay for visibility and room access, never for a list of student contacts to take away. This protects the “we never sell student data” trust claim named in the Playbook.
    • Added a Risk Register; no sponsor revenue is treated as projected or committed until cash is contracted and collected.
    • Added a weekly/between-events layer — the monthly flagships were previously the only rhythm; nothing filled the 3-4 weeks between them.
    • Founding Night and the first public event no longer both default to Raghu — venue rotates starting from the first public event.
    • Event-level KPIs are now explicitly tied to Playbook-level KPIs (Guild/Circle balance, repeat attendance, gender ratio) so both documents read from the same dashboard.

01. The Weekly Layer (runs continuously, underneath every month below)
    • Rotating Guild micro-prompt (Build/Design/Create/Hustle/Grow take turns)
    • Rotating Circle prompt/vibe-check (Foodies/Music/Sports/Travel/etc.) — pure fun, zero utility framing
    • “Collab of the Week” spotlight — a real cross-college, cross-Guild pairing that formed on CollabHub or at an event
    • Cost: ~zero. Owned by Community/Collab Lead + Content Manager.
This is what keeps the community alive between the monthly events below — without it, a 12-flagship calendar reads as isolated spikes, not a living community.

02. Month-by-Month Calendar
Month 1 — Founding Night - Invite-only, ambassador network, planning capacity subject to venue and safety checks. Internal cost only, kept minimal and approved before commitment. - Venue: Raghu is acceptable here since it’s explicitly internal/ambassador-only, not public-facing. - Goal: prove the founding cohort feels like something real, collect first 6-8 testimonial quotes (with written permission) for the website.
Month 2 — Campus Takeover #1 (first PUBLIC event) - Cross-college social hangout, free or near-free with transparent student pricing if needed, no collab-structure, no agenda pressure — let it breathe. - Venue: rotates to a different college than Month 1 — this is the first public moment and it needs to visibly not be “a Raghu thing.” - Co-organiser: host college’s student union or cultural committee (informal partnership; formal MOU tracked separately per Playbook Section 08).
Month 3 — Guild Mixer #1 - Collab-structured: skill-tag speed matching for the first 45 minutes (badge/card listing Guild + one skill + one need), open mingling after. - Cap attendance below expected demand — scarcity mechanic, not maximized headcount.
Month 4 — City Explore / Activity Day - Beach cleanup, mixed-college cricket/football, food crawl, or a trek (Simhachalam/Rushikonda). Cross-college teams by design. - Lowest cost, highest natural cross-college bonding — no forced networking required.
Month 5 — Creator Night - Content creators + video editors + graphic designers + photographers. Showcase + collab pairing (Create Guild + Design Guild). - Low-barrier ticket only if required by the approved budget. Sponsor: local cafe/co-working space as in-kind or negotiated small partner.
Month 6 — Career Connect 01 - First company-facing event. Only scheduled once real signup numbers support the pitch — do not force this to land exactly on an unproven milestone; build in a buffer month if needed. - Companies pay flat sponsorship for room access/branding — no per-lead payment for student contact data, ever (see Section 04). - Realistic framing to companies: curated shortlist from verified, opted-in students who applied for that specific event — not a purchasable database.
Month 7 — Beach Day / Social Flagship - Pure social, larger scale, low structure. This is the “screenshot and share” moment — prioritize content/photo opportunities over program agenda.
Month 8 — Overseas Education Fair - Verified study-abroad consultancies, negotiated flat sponsorship only after trust, compliance and event-readiness proof; no per-lead payment model. Consultancies pay for a stall and visibility, not a contact list.
Month 9 — Build Sprint (48-72hr cross-college challenge) - Teams must span at least 2 Guilds and 2 colleges — this is the direct operationalization of the “coder + designer + hustler” vision as a forcing function, not a hope.
Month 10 — Career Connect 02 - Second company-facing event, scaled up from Month 6 based on what actually worked. Track and report placement/internship follow-through honestly — if Month 6 produced few conversions, adjust the pitch to companies rather than repeating an unproven promise.
Month 11 — Cultural Fest / Cross-College Showcase - Music, dance, art, performance — pure Circle-driven social energy at flagship scale.
Month 12 — Clstr Crew Showcase (Annual Flagship) - Demo-day format for everything CollabHub/Guilds produced over the year, immediately followed by a social afterparty segment — the formal part proves collab works, the informal part after is what people actually post about. - Awards reward the collab, not the individual: “Best Cross-Guild Team,” “Best Cross-College Duo.”

Hard rule carried from the Community Playbook: no per-lead or per-contact payment structures, ever, from any sponsor tier. If a company or consultancy wants direct student contact, that requires a separate, explicit, event-specific opt-in at registration — never bundled into general Clstr membership or a sponsorship package.

04. Risk Register (New)
Risk
Mitigation
Title sponsor drops out close to event date
Never build an event’s core budget entirely around one title sponsor; keep In-Kind/Associate tiers as fallback coverage
Career Connect produces few/zero placements
Don’t over-promise specific placement numbers publicly before Career Connect 01 has run once; report real outcomes honestly to set expectations for Career Connect 02
Public event (Month 2+) undercuts the “cross-college, not campus-locked” identity via venue bias
Rotate host college for every public event from Month 2 onward — never repeat the same host twice in a row
Event calendar outpaces actual community readiness
Each month’s event is contingent on the prior month’s repeat-attendance and signup data, not fixed to the calendar regardless of real traction — treat this calendar as a target sequence, not a hard commitment
Gender imbalance at evening/cross-college events
Track gender ratio as a standard pre- and post-event metric (Master Logistics Checklist, Section 05); design timing and venue with this in mind
Buzz/hype mechanics perceived as try-hard by a practical, skeptical audience
If a stunt underperforms or gets called out, revert immediately to raw, low-production content — don’t double down

05. Master Logistics Checklist (Additions)
In addition to venue, budget, sponsor confirmation, and badge/printing items already tracked per event, add as standard pre-event line items: - Gender ratio target/tracking - Host college rotation check (not repeating the prior event’s host) - Guild/Circle balance of expected attendees (not just raw headcount)

06. Unified KPI Dashboard (Links to Community Playbook Section 13)
Event-level numbers (attendance, sponsor cash collected, in-kind value, tickets sold) and platform-level numbers (verified signups, meaningful activity, repeat attendance rate, Guild/Circle balance, cross-Guild collabs completed) should be read from one shared dashboard, reviewed monthly by Founder + Campus Ops Lead + Community/Collab Lead together — not as two separate reporting tracks. No event is announced until venue capacity, permissions, timing, age eligibility, safeguarding, emergency response, budget, funding fallback, registration, consent and follow-up are documented.
