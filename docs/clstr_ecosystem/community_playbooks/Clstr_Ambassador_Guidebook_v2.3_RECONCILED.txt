01
Welcome

Congratulations on being selected. This is not a certificate program. This is a real leadership role with real responsibilities, real outcomes, and real rewards — and it comes with real challenges that nobody warned you about in v1.0.

This version of the guidebook is different. It is honest. It tells you not just what to do, but what will go wrong and exactly how to handle it. Every section has been upgraded based on what Campus Captains encountered in the field.

What is Clstr?
Clstr is a Vizag-focused, cross-college student community built for students across the city. clstr.in is the supporting layer for identity, discovery, event participation and continuity. Where institutional verification is available and operating, it should be described accurately as a verification process—not as proof that every user is automatically trustworthy or safe.

What is this program about?
You are not here to forward links. You are here to own Clstr at your college — recruit, activate, run events, build a domain team, and report results. You are the founder of Clstr on your campus.

04
Domain Leads — Who Does What

Each Domain Lead has one clear area of ownership. Below is exactly what they do, what they are responsible for, and the most common failure mode in each role.

★  Clubs and Events Lead
RESPONSIBILITIES
• Onboard all active clubs onto Clstr
• Get clubs to post events on the platform
• Drive RSVP and attendance for every campus event
• Bridge between club presidents and Clstr
COMMON FAILURE + FIX
Failure: Club president agrees verbally but never posts.
Fix: Help them post the first event yourself. One completed post creates habit. Never leave it as a verbal yes.

◆  Placement and Career Lead
RESPONSIBILITIES
• Share internship and job posts in the feed
• Connect students with alumni mentors
• Promote AI career assistant to final-year students
• Coordinate with TPO office for listings
COMMON FAILURE + FIX
Failure: TPO is unresponsive or indifferent to the pitch.
Fix: Don't sell the app. Sell the outcome. Frame it as: 'Students can now access verified alumni referrals for placements without burdening your office.' TPOs care about student outcomes, not technology.

●  Community Lead
RESPONSIBILITIES
• Keep the campus feed active daily
• Encourage students to post achievements, projects, wins
• Moderate tone and quality of campus content
• Drive meaningful engagement — not just likes
COMMON FAILURE + FIX
Failure: Feed goes cold after the first week of excitement.
Fix: Create a private 'Campus Creators' WhatsApp group with 10–15 students who agree to post once a week. Give them prompts. Seed the feed yourself when it stalls. One active post resets momentum.

▲  Growth and Outreach Lead
RESPONSIBILITIES
• Run WhatsApp broadcasts and Instagram Stories
• Recruit new students to join Clstr
• Manage physical posters and QR code distribution
• Track signup numbers and report weekly
COMMON FAILURE + FIX
Failure: High QR code scans but low signup completions.
Fix: The email OTP step has friction. Stand next to the poster during high-traffic times and personally walk the first 10 students through signup. Track the actual completion rate of assisted onboarding versus passive QR scans. Do not claim a multiplier until Clstr has measured it from a defined sample.

◈  CollabHub Lead
RESPONSIBILITIES
• Promote CollabHub to students building projects
• Facilitate team formations for hackathons
• Help students create and share project portfolios
• Drive EcoCampus adoption for reuse and exchange
COMMON FAILURE + FIX
Failure: Students say 'I'll use it for the next hackathon' and never do.
Fix: Do not wait for a hackathon. Create a live Team-Up post right now for a real problem — 'Looking for a designer for a mini app project.' Show them the tool works with an active post, not a concept.

07
Real Issues You Will Face — and How to Solve Them

This section did not exist in v1.0. It exists now because every Campus Captain hits these same walls. Here they are, named honestly, with the exact fix.

ISSUE 01 — High signups, low daily active users
WHY IT HAPPENS
Students sign up to support you personally. Once they're in, there's no compelling reason to return the next day. The feed is empty. Their friends aren't posting. The platform feels dead.
THE FIX
Create one irreplaceable daily signal. The safer option is to help an authorised college or club partner share a relevant update on Clstr with written permission. Never promise exclusivity, placement access or official institutional content without approval. Retention must come from useful, voluntary community participation—not forced dependence on a single information source.

ISSUE 02 — WhatsApp group more active than the Clstr feed
WHY IT HAPPENS
WhatsApp has zero friction. Students are already there. You're asking them to open a new app to do something they can already do. The activation energy is high and the habit isn't built yet.
THE FIX
Don't fight WhatsApp. Use it as your distribution channel. Post a short summary of campus activity in WhatsApp, then add: 'Full update + RSVP on Clstr.' Redirect the existing habit toward the new platform. In 3–4 weeks, early adopters will begin posting directly on Clstr because it feels more permanent and professional.

ISSUE 03 — Domain Leads go inactive after Week 2
WHY IT HAPPENS
They signed up with enthusiasm but their role feels abstract. No clear weekly output. No immediate recognition. Exams hit. They drift. You absorb their work and burn out.
THE FIX
Make their contribution visible. Shout them out in the ambassador group when they hit a small win. Give each lead exactly ONE task for the week — not a list, one task. Review it together on the weekly call. The moment a Domain Lead's work is seen and celebrated publicly, they re-engage. Vague responsibility kills motivation. Specific visible tasks sustain it.

ISSUE 04 — Email OTP step is blocking signups
WHY IT HAPPENS
Some college email portals are slow. The OTP arrives late, or students don't check their college email habitually, or the signup flow loses them at the verification step.
THE FIX
When running live onboarding sessions, tell every student to open their college email inbox FIRST, before they even start signup. This cuts abandonment dramatically. For students whose OTP fails, flag it to the Ambassador PM immediately — this is a technical issue with a known fix. Do not let a broken OTP become the reason someone never joins.

ISSUE 05 — Clubs say yes, then do nothing
WHY IT HAPPENS
Club presidents are busy. Your request goes on a mental to-do list that never gets actioned. They said yes to you, not to the task.
THE FIX
Post their first event for them. Sit with the club president, open Clstr on your phone, and complete the event creation while they watch. The activation moment is the first post — not the agreement. After one post is live, most clubs will continue independently because they can see it working.

ISSUE 06 — You are doing everything yourself and burning out
WHY IT HAPPENS
Domain Leads are underperforming. You don't want to look bad on the leaderboard. You start absorbing their work. By Week 4 you are exhausted and inconsistent.
THE FIX
Escalate to Ambassador PM. This is what they are there for. A Campus Captain who flags a problem early and gets help is more valuable than one who silently burns out. Replace underperforming leads. Cut tasks, not quality. The program is designed for delegation — trust it.

Objection Handling — What to Say When They Say No

You will hear these exact responses. Here is what to say back. Do not memorise these word for word. Understand the logic so you can adapt naturally.

WHAT THEY SAY
WHAT YOU SAY
"We already have WhatsApp groups for this."
WhatsApp groups work until seniors graduate and the group goes silent. Clstr preserves your college's knowledge and network permanently. Your batch's opportunities stay visible to every junior who comes after you. That is not a WhatsApp group.
"I don't have time to use another app."
Clstr is not asking for time. Your campus feed is already happening — placements, events, team formations. Clstr just makes it structured and searchable instead of buried in 15 noisy groups. You spend 30 seconds, not 30 minutes.
"LinkedIn already does this."
LinkedIn doesn't know you are a CSE third-year at this college. It doesn't show you events from your campus, textbooks for sale from your seniors, or hackathon teammates from your batch. Clstr is contextual in a way LinkedIn can never be.
"I'll join after exams."
Understood. Can I help you sign up right now — just the account, takes 2 minutes — so you're in when you're ready? The feed will be live with campus activity when you return. You don't have to post anything today.
"What's the point? Nobody I know is on it."
That is exactly why it matters that you join now. Early adopters shape what the community looks like. If the people worth connecting with are on it, everyone else joins. You are that person for your batch.
"My college email doesn't work for sign-up."
Flag this to me right now. I will escalate it to the technical team today and get it fixed. This is the most common issue and it has a direct fix — your domain just needs to be whitelisted.

11
Your Path Into the Vizag Community

Campus Captain is not the ceiling — it’s the tryout. The Clstr Vizag Community is the physical, citywide network of clubs, events, and student leaders across every partner college. It runs on its own volunteer leadership layer — and that layer is recruited directly from the Ambassador Program. No new application, no separate hiring process. The best-performing Campus Captains get promoted.

L2
Campus Node Coordinator — One Per College
Promoted from the single best-performing Campus Captain at your college once you hit your milestones — not a new application. Reports to the Community/Collab Lead and keeps your college's community presence alive permanently, beyond the 90-day sprint. Recognition-based: verified ‘Community Node Lead’ badge on your Clstr profile, your name on the Vizag Community website, first access to every community event at zero ticket cost, and a signed founder recommendation letter at the 6-month mark.

This is not automatic. The role goes to Ambassadors who have already hit at least one milestone, are known and trusted on their own campus, and respond reliably within 24 hours — not to whoever simply stayed longest. Once promoted, your first task is to find at least one student from each Guild (skill-based groups — Build, Design, Career, Grow, Hustle) and each Circle (interest-based groups — Foodie, Sports, Travel, Music, Movie) at your college within the first month. This is what makes the community’s cross-skill, cross-interest promise real instead of accidental.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU RIGHT NOW
Hitting your milestones is literally the audition  •  Node Coordinators are chosen after Week 13, not during it — stay active through the finish line  •  One per college — this is competitive, not participation-based  •  Not promoted? You keep every reward you already earned as Campus Captain — nothing is at risk

Two other volunteer leadership paths exist alongside Campus Node Coordinator — Guild & Circle Leads (own a specific interest or skill group across colleges) and Community Hype & Content Creators (produce raw, campus-native content for the main community account). Both are open to any active community member, not just Ambassadors. Ask your Ambassador PM if either interests you.

14
FAQ — Honest Answers

These are the questions every Campus Captain asks. Here are the unhedged answers.

Q: How many hours per week does this actually take?
A: 3 to 5 hours if you are organised. The daily minimum is 30 minutes. The weekly report takes 15 minutes. Domain Leads handle the rest. If you are spending more than 5 hours, you are doing your Domain Leads' work for them — stop and delegate.

Q: What if a Domain Lead goes inactive?
A: Escalate to Ambassador PM immediately. You have permission to recruit a replacement without waiting for approval. Inactive leads cost you leaderboard points and momentum. Do not absorb their work — replace them.

Q: Can I run this alongside other ambassador programs?
A: Yes. But do not take this role if you cannot commit the weekly effort. One focused Campus Captain outperforms five passive ones. Quality over quantity — this applies to your own time too.

Q: What if a club president or TPO says no?
A: That is normal. Come back with the pitch deck. Focus on the outcome for them — event visibility, student engagement, NAAC reporting. Not the app. The outcome. If they say no twice, move to the next club and return to this one in 3 weeks with a case study from another club.

Q: My college email domain is not working for signup. What do I do?
A: Flag this to the Ambassador PM immediately with the full domain name. This is a whitelisting issue with a known fix. Do not let this block signups — escalate it the same day you discover it.

Q: What if I miss my Monday report?
A: Your leaderboard position drops for that week. Two consecutive misses trigger a check-in call from Ambassador PM. Three consecutive misses and your role is reviewed. The report takes 15 minutes — do not miss it.

Q: How do I get more promotional materials?
A: Request from the content team via Ambassador PM. Give 48 hours' notice. Specify: format, platform, occasion, and specific messaging needed. If you need something for tomorrow, ask today.

Q: What is the path from Ambassador to Clstr core team?
A: Two paths run in parallel. Top 3 performers at the end of each semester get priority access to internship interviews or paid campus roles at Clstr. Separately, the single best-performing Campus Captain at your college is promoted to Campus Node Coordinator — a permanent Layer 2 leadership role in the Clstr Vizag Community itself (see Section 11). Both paths are performance-based, not seniority-based. Your leaderboard rank is your application to both.

Q: What if students say there is nothing useful on the platform yet?
A: They are right — and it is your job to change that. Post the first event. Post the first job. Post the first team-up. The platform is live but your campus content is what you bring to it. You are not activating an existing community — you are building one.

Q: Can I recruit students from other colleges?
A: No. Your domain is your college only. If you want to expand to a neighbouring college, speak with the Ambassador PM — that is a separate onboarding process and requires approval.
