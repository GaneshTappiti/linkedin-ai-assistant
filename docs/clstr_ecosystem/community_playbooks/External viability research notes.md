# External viability research notes

**Reference date:** 23 August 2026.

## Local and adjacent competitors

1. **Edygrad Student Community** — https://www.edygrad.in/edygrad-community.html

Edygrad’s official community page describes a city-wide student movement in Visakhapatnam and publicly claims 5,000+ students connected, 100+ events and workshops, and 1,250+ internships offered. This is the closest local public overlap with Clstr’s broad student-community thesis. These are company claims, not independently verified figures.

2. **GDG Vizag** — https://gdg.community.dev/gdg-vizag/

The official Google Developer Groups page reports 3,241 group members and describes a technology-oriented community for people who build, create, explore technology, engineering careers and startups. This is strong evidence of an adjacent, domain-led community, not a direct proof that Clstr cannot win.

3. **TiE Vizag** — https://vizag.tie.org/

TiE Vizag’s official website frames the chapter around fostering entrepreneurship through education, networking, investing, incubation and mentoring. It is an entrepreneurship ecosystem network rather than a general student social-discovery community.

4. **Vizag Volunteers** — https://vizagvolunteers.org/

The official site describes a nonprofit organization using technology to mobilize resources for the betterment of Vizag, with public sections for projects, activities, events and donations. Its core purpose is civic/service activity rather than broad student social discovery.

5. **Solemates** — https://www.solematesrunclub.com/

The official site publicly lists run, wellness and social activity events in Vizag. Its primary identity is fitness/wellness, showing how domain communities can be active without serving the entire student category.

6. **BeInCareer** — https://beincareer.com/

The official site positions itself as a student career community and publicly claims 3,00,000+ students and 3,500+ career updates, with jobs, walk-ins, government updates, AI resume tools and skill programs. These are public company claims. Clstr should not compete on career-content volume or SEO.

7. **Student Tribe** — https://studenttribe.in/

The official site positions Student Tribe as a student marketing, campus-ambassador, gigs and upskilling platform. This confirms that app-plus-ambassador models already exist nationally; Clstr must win through deep Vizag-local community experience, social discovery and persistent cross-college continuity.

## Comparable platforms

8. **Eventria** — https://www.eventria.in/about

Eventria’s official About page positions it as a college-event and campus-event management platform with discovery, QR check-in, certificates, results, networking and gamification. It publicly states an opportunity to connect 40 million Indian college students and reports early traction claims of 200+ students connected, 5+ events hosted and 3+ colleges onboarded. These are public company claims. Eventria is a close product benchmark: Clstr cannot treat event software, cross-college events or an app alone as an automatic moat.

9. **Unstop Campus Ambassador Program** — https://unstop.com/campus-ambassador-program

Unstop’s official campus-ambassador page shows a large national student-community and ambassador model centered on networking, collaboration, opportunities and student representation. The benchmark supports the use of campus ambassadors but also shows that ambassadors need a concrete student benefit and structured operating system.

10. **Harvard Business School Online, Network Effects** — https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/what-are-network-effects

The source defines network effects as value that depends on the number of users and distinguishes direct effects among users from indirect effects between user groups. It also explains why low or free pricing can be rational early in network-effect markets. Application to Clstr: more relevant students can increase discovery value, and more students can increase partner value, but only if members repeatedly use the platform and find relevant interactions.

## Market and government sources

11. **District Visakhapatnam official profile** — https://visakhapatnam.ap.gov.in/

The official district profile reports a population of 19.595 lakh and area of 1,048 sq km. This is total population, not student population.

12. **District Colleges/Universities directory** — https://visakhapatnam.ap.gov.in/public-utility-category/colleges/

The official directory page currently lists Andhra University and GITAM under Colleges/Universities. It is not a complete citywide census of schools, colleges, universities or enrollment.

13. **AISHE Higher Education Institution Directory** — https://dashboard.aishe.gov.in/

The Government of India AISHE dashboard is an authoritative national higher-education directory and reports national survey-year enrollment, but the landing page did not expose a ready-to-use Visakhapatnam district total in the reviewed state. A defensible local TAM requires an institution-level extract or bottom-up compilation.

14. **MeitY Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025** — https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Rules-2025

The official MeitY page lists the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, published 14 November 2025, along with an enforcement timeline and Data Protection Board materials. Clstr’s no-data-sale promise should be implemented through data minimization, clear notices, purpose-specific consent, access/deletion controls, security, retention limits, role-based access and special safeguards for minors.

## Key research conclusion

The evidence supports a real competitive opportunity for a student-first, cross-college social-discovery community, but it does not support an absolute claim that no student community exists in Vizag. The strongest defensible differentiation is the combination of local student identity, broad student-life programming, campus-node distribution, and persistent clstr.in continuity. The largest unproven variables are repeat student usage, event contribution margin, repeat sponsor demand, product engagement between events, and the verified size of the local student market.


## Deep research update — contributor and volunteer program evidence

- Microsoft Learn’s Student Ambassador page describes concrete value beyond a title: practical skills, community building, short immersive rotations, direct access to teams, feedback, resume/LinkedIn stories and career resources. Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/student-hub/become-a-student-ambassador
- The U.S. National Park Service volunteer administration guidance treats volunteer programs as managed operating systems: defined roles, designated managers and supervisors, orientation/training, recognition, evaluation, secured PII records, safety/wellness support, reporting and ongoing coaching. Source: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/volunteer/rm7-ch3.htm
- Bluefield State University’s ambassador recommendation process asks references to evaluate initiative, dependability, communication, time management, leadership and concrete examples. This supports making Clstr recommendation letters evidence-based rather than automatic. Source: https://bluefieldstate.edu/student-ambassador-letter-of-recommendation/

Practical implication for Clstr: the contributor offer must provide a defined role, named supervisor, training, real outputs, feedback, documented contribution, safety rules, recognition and a transparent certificate/recommendation standard. Goodies and passes are supplementary, not the core value proposition.


## Deep research update — local student/community alternatives

- IIM Visakhapatnam’s official student-clubs page shows that even a single institution can have business, cultural, sports, social responsibility, gaming and policy bodies, plus large flagship events. This demonstrates that Clstr cannot claim students lack all community options; its opportunity is cross-college scope and city-level continuity. Source: https://www.iimv.ac.in/life-iimv/student-clubs
- GITAM Visakhapatnam’s official student-clubs page lists a wide range of cultural, technical, entrepreneurship, communication, service, media, hobby and special-interest clubs. This strengthens the need for precise positioning: Clstr should complement campus clubs rather than compete with every club inside one institution. Source: https://www.gitam.edu/visakhapatnam/campus-life/student-clubs
- Meetup’s Visakhapatnam directory displays active local groups across founders, technology, cloud, books and other interests, including groups with publicly displayed membership counts. This is evidence that city-level communities and events already exist; Clstr’s differentiation must be student identity, cross-college breadth, low-pressure belonging and continuity—not merely hosting events. Source: https://www.meetup.com/find/in--visakhapatnam/

Update implication: revise investment and positioning documents to state that the market is fragmented and populated, not empty. Clstr’s claim should be a category opportunity, not an absolute “no community exists” claim.
