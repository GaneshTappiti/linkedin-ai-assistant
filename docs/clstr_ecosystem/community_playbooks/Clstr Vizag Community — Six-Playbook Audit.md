# Clstr Vizag Community — Six-Playbook Audit

**Reference date:** 23 August 2026

## Canonical scope

All six files are about the Clstr Vizag student community. The community is the product. clstr.in is the supporting technology layer for identity, discovery, event registration, continuity and measurement. No file should present the software platform as the primary subject of the Vizag-community operating documents.

## Source files reviewed

1. `Clstr_Ambassador_Guidebook_v2.2.docx`
2. `Clstr_Vizag_Community_Playbook_v1.docx`
3. `Clstr_Vizag_Events_Playbook_12Month(1).docx`
4. `Clstr_Vizag_Community_Team_Playbook.docx`
5. `Clstr_12_Month_Events_Plan_v2.docx`
6. `Clstr_Community_Playbook_MASTER.docx`

## What is already strong

The six files consistently identify a broad student identity, the Clstr Vizag community, Circles and Guilds, cross-college activity, campus-level distribution, a weekly layer between events, a 12-month event sequence, and a no-per-lead/no-student-contact-sale principle. The Events Playbook and 12-Month Plan are substantially reconciled with one another. The Team Playbook adds a useful two-layer organization and founder-delegation model. The Ambassador Guidebook adds field-level objections, weekly actions and operational problems that the strategic documents did not capture.

## Material contradictions to fix

| Issue | Files affected | Correct canonical rule |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute category claim | Master says Clstr is “the only entity in Vizag.” Ambassador Guidebook says “India’s first verified cross-college campus network.” | Use: “Clstr is building Vizag’s cross-college student social-discovery community.” If “first” is used, qualify the exact category and use “to our knowledge” until independently proven. |
| Community versus platform framing | Ambassador Guidebook describes Clstr primarily as an India-wide campus network/platform. | For Vizag materials, say: “Clstr is the student community; clstr.in is the layer that supports it.” |
| Fabricated-looking public counter | Master includes “2,847 verified students” as a buzz example. | Remove all example numbers that could be mistaken for live traction. Use a dynamic placeholder only in an implementation template, never in public copy. |
| Market-size claims | Older source materials imply very large student denominators and broad reach without a reproducible source. | Treat 131 institutions, 100,000 engineering students and 200,000–300,000 total students as unverified hypotheses. Use an institution-level database before publishing numbers. |
| Ambassador targets | Guidebook targets 500 verified users at 30 days and 2,000 per college, while the wider playbooks frame founding-stage behavior gates and small cohorts. | Treat 500/2,000 as stretch targets, not promises or definitions of success. Core score is verified activation, meaningful activity, repeat attendance and retention. |
| Ambassador KPI quality | Guidebook lists 10+ events per month and 2,000 verified users as headline indicators, plus 60% RSVP attendance. | Replace headline KPIs with active-node output, verified meaningful participation, repeat attendance, cross-college activity, safety and retention. Event count is secondary. |
| Incentive conflict | Master lists ₹2,000–3,000/month Campus Captain stipends and ₹15,000–20,000/month Community Lead pay. Team Playbook and Ambassador Guidebook state no fixed cash promises because there is no funding. | Team Playbook canon wins: no fixed cash promise now. Any future pay or revenue share requires actual surplus, written terms, team approval and financial capacity. Update Master. |
| Career claims | Guidebook suggests TPOs could post placement shortlists/exclusive deadlines and promotes an AI career assistant. | Never promise exclusivity or institutional access without written permission. Career is a later community program, not the initial retention dependency. |
| Contact and platform use | Guidebook requires daily feed activity, private WhatsApp creator groups and campus broadcasts; wider documents say clstr.in should create continuity. | WhatsApp/Instagram are distribution channels; clstr.in is the official community record and follow-up destination. Do not let external channels become the real product. |
| Timing definitions | Events documents define Month 1 as Phase 1 after roughly two months of Phase 0; Team Playbook calls Community/Collab Lead Month 1 and Node Coordinators Month 2. | Use one timeline: Phase 0 = Weeks 1–6/8 founding validation; Phase 1 = founding/ambassador activation; Phase 2 = first public event; Phase 3 = measured Vizag scaling. Label calendar months relative to the phase, not as fixed promises. |
| Node-role overlap | Ambassador Guidebook uses Campus Captain as the main campus leader and Team Playbook uses Campus Node Coordinator as a promoted Layer 2 role; Master mixes the terms. | Campus Captain = ambassador-program role during activation. Campus Node Coordinator = promoted, ongoing community role after performance proof. Keep Ambassador Program Manager separate from Community/Collab Lead. |
| Team size and hiring | Team Playbook says 7 roles, but several roles overlap and multiple full-time-like tasks are assigned before funding. | Keep seven functional areas but allow one person to hold multiple roles. Do not hire fixed payroll before recurring contribution supports it. |
| Content KPI | Team Playbook requires daily posting and zero missed days, while community strategy prioritizes authentic content and weekly activity. | Measure meaningful shares, replies, member stories, event conversion and retention. Consistency matters, but daily posting is not a business outcome by itself. |
| Sponsorship pricing | Older Master and 12-Month Plan contain fixed price bands; Events Playbook intentionally removes fixed cash figures. | Use qualitative tiers in strategic playbooks; maintain internal price tests separately. Never present price bands as validated willingness-to-pay. |
| Sponsorship stage | Events files correctly stage in-kind first, then cash after 2–3 successful events; Master retains a broader revenue ladder. | Adopt staged commercialization: no sponsor during founding proof; in-kind during early public proof; paid pilot only after delivery evidence; renewal before annual partner. |
| Evidence language | Master describes direct research and competitor metrics as facts; other research establishes that some numbers are public company claims. | Label sourced government facts, public company claims, internal observations, hypotheses and targets separately. |
| Safety and minors | Community files mention curfews, gender ratios and parental/warden permission but do not consistently define age eligibility, safeguarding or consent. | Start with adult college students unless a minors policy, parental consent, supervision, photo controls and sponsor restrictions are implemented. |
| KPI source of truth | Events documents specify a unified dashboard; Ambassador Guidebook uses a separate tracker/leaderboard. | One shared source of truth, with role-specific views. Leaderboards cannot override safety, retention or quality metrics. |

## Canonical community operating model

The community has one promise: help students meet, participate and build beyond their own college. It has two participation tracks: Circles for low-pressure interests and belonging, and Guilds for skill/collaboration. Events are physical expressions of the community. clstr.in provides persistent identity, discovery, registration, attendance history, connections and follow-up. Campus nodes provide trusted local distribution and insight. Partners fund relevant experiences and receive aggregate outcomes, not student contact lists.

## Canonical evidence rules

The six files must never present the following as established facts without updated evidence: total Vizag student population, total institutions, literal first/only status, 500–5,000 event demand, 2,000 users per college, 10+ events per month, sponsor willingness-to-pay, placement outcomes, exclusive TPO access, or annual sponsor commitments.

The six files may present the following as strategy, not proof: a large multi-institution student opportunity, domain-agnostic programming, campus ambassadors, event sponsorship, cross-college identity, and a possible Vizag-to-other-cities expansion path.

## Update priority

1. Update the Master Playbook first; it must become the source of truth.
2. Update the Ambassador Guidebook to remove platform-first and fixed-target overclaims, and to align roles with Captain → Node Coordinator progression.
3. Update the Team Playbook to remove any ambiguity about fixed pay, employment status, authority, safety and founder dependency.
4. Update the Events Playbook and 12-Month Plan to align language, timeline labels, KPI definitions, safety checklist and price-band treatment.
5. Update the older Community Playbook v1 as a clearly superseded archive or replace it with a reconciled version.
6. Maintain one shared claim register, KPI dictionary, event checklist, sponsor terms template and community calendar.

## Final audit conclusion

The source set is strategically thoughtful but not yet a clean operating system. Its strongest material is the student-first identity, Circles/Guilds balance, field-level ambassador problems, event readiness logic, no-data-sale rule and two-layer team model. Its main weaknesses are overconfident claims, target/KPI inflation, fixed-pay contradictions, category overlap with existing communities, mixed timeline definitions and insufficiently explicit trust controls. The correct update is not to make the plan smaller; it is to make every promise testable.
